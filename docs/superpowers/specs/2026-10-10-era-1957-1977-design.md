- **Sibling:** [the Yishuv design](2026-10-10-era-yishuv-design.md) (#49). Its section layout is followed here.
- **Parent spec:** [2026-10-09-statecraft-israel-design.md](2026-10-09-statecraft-israel-design.md) (§1 perspective and
  owner decisions; §3 systems, the ladder and intelligence uncertainty; §4 loop; §5 content pipeline)

This document says what the player does from the aftermath of Sinai to the change of government of 1977, and how each
system of today's sim serves that period, by reuse or by a stated new piece. It begins at the Founding chapter's end on
1 January 1957 and ends where #51 begins. It writes no code and no content. The work items are listed in §12 and
drafted as issues in "Detailed issues".

Facts quoted here size the design. Each one names its source and the day it was checked. A fact marked *to verify*
has no checked source yet, or its sources disagree. The research issues (§12) settle it before any content uses it.

**Decisions.** Eleven owner decisions, D1–D11, are listed with their recommended defaults in §13. The rest of the
document assumes the defaults. The two that most change the build are D3 (adding this era moves `Hash1957` once) and
D9 (the map today does not reach the Suez Canal).

## 1. The rules this design keeps

1. **The spec's perspective rule** (spec §1; `docs/CONTENT_POLICY.md` §1). Narration and terms are Israeli-perspective,
   from mainstream Israeli historiography, with one sourced value per fact and no opposing-narrative panels. Arab
   states, the Palestinian organisations, the superpowers and the UN are AI actors with their own goals.
2. **What holds regardless** (CONTENT_POLICY §2): every fact is sourced; no quotes are invented; nothing rewards an
   atrocity; the Holocaust (here the Eichmann trial) is reacted to and never played, and is shown without swastikas;
   real people appear in their public roles.
3. **No reset.** The Founding chapter carries into this one by the chapters design's deviation carry (its D1).
4. **The 1948–52 goldens do not move.** `GoldenRun.Hash1950`, `Hash1952` and `SMOKE OK 5 turns` stay the same through
   every item in §12. `Hash1957` moves exactly once, on purpose, in the item that adds the era (D3, §9.2).
5. **Data decides, code implements** (chapters design §1.4, §5). This era adds **no new `systems` switch and no new
   engine abstraction** in its first build. Everything it needs is either in the chapters items (#56–#67), in the M4
   systems it reuses (#21 war mode, #22 defence depth, #11 and the M4 plan's N5 diplomacy standing, the M4 plan's N11
   dated rules, #19 legacy report, #23 map actions), or in content. The two places where a new piece could be argued
   for (a map region for settlement actions, a larger map) are owner decisions D6 and D9, with the smaller option
   recommended.
6. **The reference run is history.** A policy's `default` is its level at the start of its window. Each later
   historical change is a hard event's `set_policy` on a sourced date, so the `HistoricalAI` reproduces history
   without sending policy orders (as in the Yishuv design §1.7).
7. **M4's sensitive-content rules extend here** (M4 D8; §10). Massacres and terror against Israelis are react-only.
   Israeli operations are decided at the level of policy, doctrine or operation, never at the level of civilian harm.
   Israeli acts that killed civilians are on an owner include/omit list, and nothing is omitted without the owner.

## 3. The era

### 3.1 The `content/eras.yaml` record (D1)

```yaml
  - id: sinai_to_upheaval           # D1: id and name are owner choices
    name: From Sinai to the Upheaval
    from: 1957-01-01                # the Founding era's `to` (chapters design D3)
    to: 1977-06-20                  # Begin forms the 18th government (§2.1; the day is D10)
    player_office: [{ from: 1957-01-01, office: prime_minister }]   # the Yishuv design's key, if adopted (#56)
    systems:
      legislature: state
      map_actions: [housing]        # plus #23's actions when they land; transit camps close by about 1963 (§2.4)
    start: start/sinai_to_upheaval.yaml
    carry: carry/sinai_to_upheaval.yaml
```

- **No new switch.** `war_mode` is the Yishuv design's key with a default of "on" (#81); this era leaves it at the
  default. `economy: state` is today's. Founding war mode (#21) is gated on rung 5, which this era reaches in 1967 and
  1973.
- **`rules/sinai_to_upheaval.yaml`** holds only what this era changes: its economy values (§5.3), and nothing else in
  the first build. Changes **inside** the era (the 1962 and later devaluations, the 1966 recession, the 1973 shock) are
  dated rule values (the M4 plan's N11), not era boundaries.
- **Why one era, not two.** A boundary at June 1967 was considered (a "pre-war" and a "post-war" chapter). Rules do
  not change at 1967: the same state, the same Knesset, the same ministries and the same turn grid. The territories are
  content windows (`from: 1967-06-05`), not a new rule set. One era keeps one calibration and one carry file. The
  chapter is long (about 82 quarters plus war turns), which parts (§3.2) and the legacy report at the end make
  readable.
- **Why the end is the government, not the election.** #51 starts "from the 1977 change of government". The election of
  17 May 1977 is this chapter's last hard anchor; the chapter ends when the new government takes office, so the
  coalition talks of May–June 1977 are played under the outgoing government, and the next chapter opens with the new
  one in office.

| **A. 20 June 1977, the new government (recommended)** | A real change of office, matching #51's wording; the election falls inside this chapter | The day is disputed by one (20 or 21 June; D10) |

## 4. Who the player is (D2)

The player is the **Prime Minister**, as in the Founding chapter. Five holders serve in this chapter (§2.1). The player
plays the office, not a person or a party:

- **Changes of Prime Minister** (Ben-Gurion's resignation in June 1963, Eshkol's death in February 1969, the interim
  Allon, Meir's resignation in April 1974, Rabin's withdrawal as list head in April 1977) are hard events. In
  Historical mode each happens on its date. Each sets new `people.yaml` terms; `Cabinet.ExpireOnGovernmentChange` ends
  the player's appointments when the government record changes, as today.
- **Elections** (1959, 1961, 1965, 1969, 1973, 1977) are hard events whose historical outcome sets the next
  `legislatures.yaml` record, as `elections_1949` does today.
- **The 1977 election does not end the playthrough in Historical mode.** The spec says losing the premiership through
  an election ends a playthrough (spec §4). In Historical mode the 1977 election is a hard anchor; its result is history,
  and the chapter ends with the legacy report (#19) on 20 June 1977, then the game goes on into #51 with the office in
  new hands. A collapse caused by the player (coalition stability below the threshold, or #8's military or economic
  collapse) still ends the playthrough, as today. The Open-mode version, where 1977 can go either way, is #10's.

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The office, Historical mode keeps history's transitions (recommended)** | No new rule; the Founding chapter already changes holders (Ben-Gurion, Sharett, Ben-Gurion); #51 can begin | The player "loses" in 1977 by history, not by play. The legacy report is where the player's own record is judged |
| B. The player is the Labor leadership; 1977 ends the game | A party story | Breaks the chain into #51; a party is not an office (spec §1 real offices) |
| C. The 1977 election is decided by play in Historical mode too | Agency | Historical mode would no longer reproduce history; this is Open mode (#10) |

**Offices.** No new office is needed in the first build. The Founding offices continue: `prime_minister`,
`defense_minister`, `finance_minister`, `foreign_minister`, `chief_of_staff` and the rest. Two are proposed for the
defence system, each only if #22's design wants it: `intelligence_chief` (director of Aman: spec §3 names "the
intelligence chief") and `mossad_director`. Their holders are research (§12, item 1). Offices that end or begin
(Ministry of Development, Ministry of Tourism and others, *to verify*) get `from`/`until` windows (#57).

## 6. Intelligence uncertainty and 1973 (D5)

Spec §3 says the player sees an **estimate** with uncertainty that can be wrong. This chapter is where that rule
matters most. It uses #22's intelligence part; it does not build its own.

What this chapter needs from #22's design (stated as requirements, not as a design of #22):

1. **An estimate, not the truth.** Each turn the Defence screen shows the intelligence chief's estimate of the main
   threats (for example "probability of war on the southern front this quarter: low"), with a stated confidence. The
   true state is in the sim and is never shown.
2. **Historical estimates are data.** On the reference run, the estimate on a date is the sourced historical one: for
   example "low probability" in the first days of October 1973 (the Agranat interim report, *to verify* the wording in
   a source that gives it). A dated `assessments` list in content gives these values, with sources.
3. **The estimate's quality follows the player's choices.** Policies and appointments (the intelligence chief's
   competence, which is a game-balance value) move its error band. The error itself is drawn from the seeded RNG, so
   it is deterministic.
4. **Warnings are events.** The May 1973 warning and the warning of the night of 5–6 October are soft and hard events
   whose options are the government's real choices: call up, partial call-up, wait. Each option costs money and
   standing (the US) in its own way.
5. **History happens in Historical mode.** The war begins on 6 October 1973 whatever the player chose. What the
   player's choices change is the force balance on the day, through mobilisation, readiness and pre-emption (war mode
   reads them).
6. **No date leak.** No player-facing text before 6 October names the war's date. The chapter card names parts by
   their start dates only when they begin (§3.2).

The Agranat Commission (21 November 1973) is a hard event with the government's real responses. Its interim report's
recommendations (§2.1) change appointments by `people.yaml` terms on their dates.

**1967.** The same mechanism shows the waiting period: the estimate of Egyptian intentions in May 1967, the
mobilisation's daily cost to the economy, and the choice to wait or strike.

| Option for D5 | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **A. #22's estimate system, with historical estimates as dated data (recommended)** | Spec §3; deterministic; one system for every era | Depends on #22, which is "if ready" in M4 |
| B. Events only: warnings and choices, no standing estimate | Buildable now | The player never sees an estimate; 1973 becomes a scripted surprise |
| C. A special 1973 surprise rule | Simple | A one-off rule in code for one date breaks "data decides" |

### 10.3 Violence against civilians: five rules (D7, extending M4 D8)

1. **Operations are decided at the level of policy, doctrine or operation.** The strikes on the diversion works,
   Samu, Karameh, the Beirut airport raid (December 1968, *to verify*), the deep raids, the operations after Munich and
   Entebbe are each an order at the level the government gave it, with the sourced military aim. Civilian deaths are
   never an option and never rewarded. Where civilians were killed, the event carries `war-casualties` (or `massacre`
   if the owner so rules) and states the sourced fact.
2. **Attacks on Israelis are react-only.** Avivim, Lod, Munich, Kiryat Shmona, Ma'alot and the others in §10.2 are
   events the government responds to; the options are its real responses (negotiation, rescue, the order to strike back
   at the policy level) and their alternatives. No option rewards the attacker's act or an indiscriminate response.
3. **Israeli acts that killed civilians or were found to be wrong are on an owner list,** each with include, omit or
   how-to-show. Research item 6 drafts it with dates and one sourced fact per item. Candidates (dates from §2 where
   checked, else *to verify*):
   - the civilian deaths at Samu (13 Nov 1966);
   - the Moroccan Quarter demolition (from 10 Jun 1967);
   - the Latrun villages in 1967 and the flight of residents from the West Bank and the Golan in 1967 (*to verify*;
     war mode never models these, by M4 D4(e));
   - the killing of Egyptian prisoners in 1967, as reported in mainstream Israeli sources (*to verify*);
   - the USS Liberty (June 1967, *to verify* the date);
   - Bahr el-Baqar (8 Apr 1970);
   - the Libyan airliner (21 Feb 1973);
   - Lillehammer (21 Jul 1973);
   - Land Day (30 Mar 1976);
   - the military government over Arab citizens until 1 Dec 1966 (already on M4's list).
4. **Settlement and the territories are shown as policy, neutrally** (§7.2): options are the government's real ones;
   consequences are sourced (standing with the US and the UN, security effects, cost); no option is labelled right or
   wrong, and none is rewarded for its ideology. The legal advice the cabinet received on Kfar Etzion (§2.5) is shown
   as a sourced fact, not as a verdict.
5. **No omission bypasses the owner.** Leaving a candidate out needs the owner's explicit approval, as Lydda and Ramle
   did; the list is recorded in this chapter's release notes, as M4's is in `docs/m4-release.md`.

| B. Leave the Israeli acts out by default | Less review | A gap the record does not have; rule 5 forbids it without the owner |
| C. Let the player order the acts | — | Breaks "nothing rewards an atrocity" |
