# Iraqi airlift (Operation Ezra and Nehemiah): the dates (research note, 2026-10-13)

Status: research only. Nothing here changes `content/`. Refs #135 (spot-check item 6: the 1950 phase of the Iraqi
emigration).

The question: Hebrew Wikipedia puts the first flights in mid-May 1950; English Wikipedia says "mid-May 1951". This
note settles it from scholarly and primary sources, and records the law's dates, monthly counts, the end date and the
total.

How it was done:
- Every page was read on 13 October 2026. Wikipedia was read through the MediaWiki API and used only as a pointer to
  sources.
- JTA reports were read on jta.org. For the first-flight report I also read the scanned JTA Daily News Bulletin
  (pdfs.jta.org) to get the dateline.
- Devorah Hacohen's chapter and Daphna Tsimhoni's article were read in the Internet Archive copies of the MATAH
  virtual library (lib.cet.ac.il has been taken down).
- Not reached: the Meir-Glitzenstein PDF that `iraqi_exodus.yaml` cites (in.bgu.ac.il returns a bot challenge), NLI
  JPress (403), and the full texts of Gat 1997, Shiblak 1986, Hillel 1987 and Meir-Glitzenstein 2004 (Internet Archive
  lending copies, no search access). Where I use those books, it is through a quotation in another source, and I say
  so.
- [C] marks a conflict between sources. "Unverified" means I found the claim but could not read the source behind it.

**The first flights were in May 1950, not May 1951.** Every scholarly and contemporary source I read agrees on 1950.
English Wikipedia's "mid-May 1951" is uncited, tagged "citation needed" since March 2022, and goes back to the article's
first revision in 2005, which also dated the emigration law to 1951 (see "Where the 1951 error comes from").

## 1. The Denaturalisation Law, March 1950

- **Number and form.** Law No. 1 of 1950, passed as an annex to the Denaturalisation Law No. 62 of 1933 so it could go
  through by emergency procedure. Jews who wished could leave Iraq for good after giving up their Iraqi nationality.
  The law ran for one year, the Minister of the Interior carried it out, and it could be cancelled at any time by royal
  decree. Tsimhoni (below), first section.
- **Passage.** [C] on the exact days:
  - Tsimhoni: passed "in secret and in haste" by both houses of parliament on 2–4 March 1950.
  - JTA, 6 March 1950: the Chamber of Deputies had passed the bill, citing a Times report from Baghdad "this
    week-end"; Senate passage was "considered certain".
    https://www.jta.org/archive/iraq-lower-house-passes-bill-permitting-jews-to-renounce-citizenship-leave-country
  - JTA, 7 March 1950: Iraq had announced that Jews "would be permitted to leave the country within a period of one
    year". https://www.jta.org/archive/british-controlled-arab-radio-praises-iraq-decision-to-permit-emigration-of-jews
  - Adam Shatz (London Review of Books, 6 November 2008, a review essay, not scholarship) calls it "the Denaturalisation
    Law of 4 March 1950". https://www.lrb.co.uk/the-paper/v30/n21/adam-shatz/leaving-paradise
  - Hacohen: the bill was tabled by the Interior Minister, Salih Jabr, "at the beginning of March 1950".
  - Hebrew Wikipedia ("מבצע עזרא ונחמיה"): put to the Chamber on 3 March, to the Senate the next day, and "published
    on 9 March 1950, Purim". Unverified: no source is given for 9 March, and Purim 1950 fell on 3 March, so the
    sentence does not hold together as written.
  - Recommendation: say "early March 1950" in game text. If a day is needed, 4 March 1950 (passage) has the most
    support. The gazette date is unverified.
- **Registration term.** One year. The Israeli Foreign Ministry told the UN Conciliation Commission that the asset-freeze
  bill came on 10 March 1951, "the day after the expiry of the term for registration". That puts the end of the term on
  9 March 1951. Letter dated 19 March 1951, Director General of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Israel to the UNCCP
  chairman (UN doc. A/AC.25/IS.60). https://www.un.org/unispal/document/auto-insert-211728
  - [C] Shatz: "By 8 March, when the deadline was due to expire". Gat, as quoted in the English Wikipedia article on the
    Baghdad bombings, also counts registrations "by 8 March". These differ by a day, depending on whether the term is
    counted to 8 or 9 March.
  - [C] JTA, 19 January 1951, citing Near East Radio: Iraq announced the end of emigration of Israel-bound Jews "as of
    March 1, 1951". https://www.jta.org/archive/iraq-to-ban-immigration-of-jews-after-february-92000-iraqi-jews-may-lose-citizenship
    The other sources show registration ran until 8 or 9 March, so this was an announcement that did not hold.

## 2. The first flights, May 1950

- **Ben-Porat's account** (Mordechai Ben-Porat, *Le-Baghdad ve-hazara* (To Baghdad and Back), Ma'ariv 1996, pp. 96–97,
  as quoted on he.wiki; the book itself not read): on Friday 19 May 1950 at 14:00 a Skymaster with 86 immigrants and an
  Iraqi police officer took off from Baghdad for Nicosia, landed there at 15:50, and reached Lydda at 18:00. A second
  plane landed soon after with 89 immigrants. 175 in all. In May 1950, the first month, 614 Jews came in six flights.
  19 May 1950 was a Friday, which fits.
- **JTA Daily News Bulletin, Monday 22 May 1950, p. 2**, dateline "TEL AVIV, May 21": "The first group of Iraqi Jews to
  be evacuated to Israel arrived here today by air aboard two giant skymasters. The Jews, numbering 170 men, women and
  children, stopped at Cyprus en route to Lydda." The airlift, which "must be completed within one year", is called
  "Operation Ali Baba" (the web text has "All Baba"; the scan reads "Ali Baba").
  https://www.jta.org/archive/first-group-of-jews-evacuated-from-iraq-to-israel-arrives-at-lydda-numbers-170 (scan:
  http://pdfs.jta.org/1950/1950-05-22_098.pdf)
- **Hacohen (scholarly):** "On 21 May 1950 the newspapers reported the first group of immigrants who came from Iraq by
  air to Lydda: this was the start of Operation Ezra and Nehemiah." Her footnote 51 cites *Yedioth Ahronoth*, 21 May
  1950.
- **Shatz (LRB):** Shlomo Hillel ("Richard Armstrong") flew into Baghdad on 27 April 1950 for Near East Air Transport
  (NEAT), and NEAT signed its contract with the Iraqi government on 9 May 1950. Hacohen has the deal agreed "at the
  beginning of May". British permission for the Cyprus stop was needed and obtained.
- **[C] Arrival day.** Ben-Porat has the planes landing on 19 May. The JTA dateline says 21 May ("arrived here today"),
  and the English Wikipedia bombings article says 20 May (citing the Chicago Tribune of 22 May 1950, not read; it was the
  source for "46,000 registered" too). A likely reading, which is my inference: the planes landed on Friday evening 19
  May. Saturday 20 May was the Sabbath, when Israeli papers did not appear, so the news ran on Sunday 21 May, and the
  JTA wire took that date. Ben-Porat was an organiser writing from the operation's records, but I read him only through
  he.wiki. **Use 19 May 1950 for the first flight, and "May 1950" wherever a day is not needed.**
- **Route.** The first flights went via Nicosia (Cyprus). By 15
  March 1951 the planes were "flying the direct Bagdad-Lydda route" (JTA, 15 March 1951, below). I did not find the day
  the direct flights began [GAP].

## 4. The asset freeze, 10 March 1951

- 10 March 1951: the government put a bill to the Chamber freezing all assets of Jews who had given up their
  citizenship. It passed the same day and the Regency Council gave its assent at once. The banks closed for two days,
  and police closed Jewish shops, including those of Jews not registered. Israeli letter to the UNCCP, 19 March 1951,
  paragraphs 3–4.
- FRUS 1951 V, doc. 340, editors' note: "On March 10, the Iraqi Government had frozen the assets of those Iraqi Jews who had
  registered for emigration from Iraq to Israel."
  https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1951v05/d340
- Hacohen: on 10 March 1951 the Chamber in Baghdad passed the freeze law; by then 103,866 had given up their nationality.
- [C] Shatz: the freeze came "the next day" after 8 March, which would be 9 March. The primary sources give 10 March.

## 5. The end

- **Deadlines after the freeze.** FRUS 1951 V, doc. 340 (7 May 1951) has the State Department asking Iraq to extend
  "the May 31 deadline of Jewish emigration from Iraq in order that the airlift program could be completed". The editors' notes
  quote a telegram from Baghdad of 2 June 1951: "NEAT rep states airlift denationalized Jews to Israel will be
  completed by end of June", and a Tel Aviv report of 6 July 1951 that "the movement of 106,662 Jews from Iraq to
  Israel would be completed in the near future". Hacohen: the press gave 18 July 1951 as the final date for departures.
- **Bulk done by mid-1951.** Hacohen: 103,000 had come from Iraq by the end of June 1951.
- **Operation's end.** [C]
  - Ben-Porat (as quoted on he.wiki): the operation lasted until January 1952, "exactly twenty months", from May 1950.
  - English Wikipedia: "early 1952" (citation needed).
  - Shatz: "By the end of 1952, almost all of Iraq's Jews had fled."
  - he.wiki (unverified): Israel ordered the activity in Iraq stopped on 15 June 1952, after the arrests of May 1951.
  - Recommendation: the airlift ended in January 1952. That is just inside the MVP window, which runs to January 1952.

## 6. The total

- Ben-Porat (as quoted on he.wiki): 110,618 Iraqi Jews came by air in 900 flights, and 9,352 escaped through Iran;
  119,970 in all, out of a community of 138,000. (110,618 + 9,352 = 119,970.) He.wiki also mentions 8,000 Jews of
  Persian origin who left with Persian passports; they are not in the 119,970.
- Tsimhoni: 124,000 gave up their nationality and went to Israel within a year and a half; about 6,000 Jews stayed.
- CBS, *Immigration to Israel 2002–2006* (Publication 1365), as already recorded in
  `research_notes/M3 content 1950-51/facts.md` (section 4b): 123,371 immigrants born in Iraq, 15 May 1948 to 1951. This
  includes those who left before the law.
- [C] So the total depends on what is counted: about 110,000 by the airlift itself, about 120,000 for the whole
  1950–52 emigration including Iran, and up to 124,000 by renunciations.

## Where the 1951 error comes from

- The sentence "The first planes flew to Israel via Cyprus in mid-May 1951" is in the first revision of the English
  Wikipedia article (revision 23544241, 19 September 2005). That revision also says the Iraqi government "passed a
  special bill permitting their emigration in 1951". So the year was wrong for the whole sequence from the start, not a
  slip on the flight date alone. The 2005 text gives no source; I could not trace where it came from.
- The Jewish Virtual Library page "Operation Ezra & Nehemia" has the same paragraphs, with "in 1951, the Iraqi
  government passed a special bill" and "beginning on May 18, 1951". It lists Wikipedia among its sources, so it is not
  independent. https://www.jewishvirtuallibrary.org/operation-ezra-and-nehemia-the-airlift-of-iraqi-jews
- Two things may have kept the error alive, though I cannot show they caused it. The neighbouring sentence "In March
  1951, the Israeli government organized an airlift" cites Meir-Glitzenstein 2004, p. 203, and she does put the mass
  airlift in 1951. And Meir-Glitzenstein (pp. 208–209, as quoted on en.wiki) writes "mid-May 1951" for the arrest of
  the emissaries Ben-Porat and Tajer, a different event.
- The English article's lead ("from 1951 to 1952") and its "Airlift" section repeat the error. The English article on
  the Baghdad bombings has the right date ("20 May 1950").
