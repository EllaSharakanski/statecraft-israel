# Budget approval, 1948-1956: evidence ledger

Checked 11 October 2026; the 1949/50, 1950/51 and 1951/52 passage records were added on 14 October 2026 (see "Final votes read from the plenum protocols"). Early research draft for #3, not a completed annual vote catalogue. No `budget_approval` or `open_questions` value is changed. The exact vote margins still missing, and the missing annual laws, remain open below.

Tags used in the 14 October 2026 additions: **verified** (two independent primary records agree, or the protocol and the Knesset OData record agree), **single source**, **conflicting** (both values given) and **not found** (with what was searched).

## What the sources establish

### Provisional State Council

The original Law and Administration Ordinance, No. 1 of 1948, chapter 2 section 5, says that the Provisional Government's budget is fixed by an ordinance of the Provisional State Council. Section 7 identifies that council as the legislative authority. This is direct legal evidence of a budget-approval role, even though `provisional_state_council.budget_approval` currently reads `false` in the game. Whether that boolean represents legislative authority or a particular game mechanic needs a separate maintainer decision; this note changes neither.

Primary source: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/0/law/0_lsr_312670.pdf (sections 5 and 7, Hebrew text). An English transcription is available at https://palquest.org/en/historictext/16010/israel-law-and-administration-ordinance . The latter is a mirror, not the official publication.

The Provisional State Council's sitting 23, 14 October 1948, has a contents entry for approval of the July-December 1948 budget and its ordinance (printed pages 7-43): https://fs.knesset.gov.il/0/Plenum/0_ptm_249991.pdf . The contents list item B as the end of the debate on the six-month state budget and its approval (pages 7-42) and item C as approval of the budget ordinance (pages 42-43). On printed page 42 the chair, Yosef Sprinzak, puts the whole budget, as proposed by the Finance Committee, to a single vote: "נגד התקציב — 3, נמנע — 1, בעד התקציב — 28" (against 3, abstaining 1, for 28). Just before, Berl Repetur's amendment was rejected 2 for, 15 against, 10 abstaining. The council then moved on to the budget ordinance (July-December 1948) itself. Checked against the page image in review, 11 October 2026. Kaplan's contemporaneous speech, reproduced at https://benyehuda.org/read/34720 , describes the July-December 1948 budget as current revenues/expenditures plus a separate war budget. Do not conflate this six-month proposal with a later full fiscal-year law.

Knesset's own budget-history page says the first orderly budget proposal was for January-March 1949 under the Provisional State Council, followed by the first full-year proposal for 1949/50. It also notes frequent supplementary budgets in the first decade. This is an official retrospective, not the January-March ordinance itself: https://main.knesset.gov.il/About/Pages/Budget/BudgetHistory.aspx . Do not infer a vote date or tally from it, or turn "first orderly proposal" into "first budget ever": the 1948 primary record above is earlier.

A separate election appropriation demonstrates an actual council budget ordinance: the Constituent Assembly Elections Budget Ordinance, No. 46 of 1949, authorises 120,000 pounds and divides it in a schedule. It is dated 20 January 1949. It is an election-specific appropriation, not the general January-March budget: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/0/law/0_lsr_312736.PDF .

### First Knesset

The Transition Law, 1949, sections 1-2, calls the legislature the Knesset, the Constituent Assembly the First Knesset, and its legislation a law. Its publication footnote records adoption on 16 February 1949: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/law/1_lsr_208911.pdf .

Finance Minister Eliezer Kaplan's 1949/50 budget speech explicitly introduces the first whole-year budget and asks the Knesset to give the Finance Committee authority to approve the defence budget. This is a primary speech reproduced in his collected writings, not a roll-call or enacted-law text: https://benyehuda.org/read/34722 .

Official errata independently corroborate that the 1949/50 Budget Law was published in *Sefer HaHukim* 23 on 9 September 1949, citing corrections to pages 209, 215 and 216: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/law/1_lsr_ec_317740.pdf . The errata establish publication and the law's existence, not its full fiscal-date clause, passage date or vote tally. The enacted text, its adoption date and the passage tally were read on 14 October 2026; see the next section. No corrected appropriation amount is imported into game data. Readable official text checked 11 October 2026; scan pixels were not available here.

The First Knesset's sitting 159 record shows the 1950/51 budget in second and third readings, with objections to ministry allocations voted on and rejected and committee-text sections adopted. The portion read includes Welfare, Labour and Immigration. The same PDF ends with the summaries of sittings 156-159. Sitting 159 (14 Tammuz 5710, 29 June 1950) records that the expenditure budget and then the 1950/51 Budget Law itself were adopted in third reading in the Finance Committee's wording. The summary gives no tally: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/Plenum/1_ptm_250044.pdf . (Summary page checked against the page image in review, 11 October 2026.) The body of the same protocol does record the tallies; see the next section. Rejected opposition amendments are not a failed government budget.

### Final votes read from the plenum protocols (14 October 2026)

All three protocols below were downloaded directly from fs.knesset.gov.il on 14 October 2026. Each tally was read in the PDF text layer and then checked against the page image; the printed page is the Divrei HaKnesset page number at the top of the scan. The sitting for each law was found through the Knesset OData service (`KNS_PlmSessionItem` for the bill, then `KNS_DocumentPlenumSession` for the protocol file). Thanks to @agamrafaeli-instinct, whose note on #3 mapped which First Knesset protocol ids (`1_ptm_250001` to `250075`) were unreadable from outside Israel and where the readable text of sitting 159 stopped (section 20). That map set the search.

**1949/50 Budget Law: passed 31 August 1949, sitting 74 of the First Knesset.**

- Date and sitting: **verified.** The protocol heading reads "הישיבה השבעים־וארבע של הכנסת הראשונה", 6 Elul 5709 (31 August 1949), Tel Aviv, 12.00, item A "חוק התקציב לשנת 1949/50, תש"ט־1949" (printed page 1459). The enacted law ends "נתקבל בכנסת ביום ו' באלול תש"ט (31 באוגוסט 1949)", read on the page image: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/law/1_lsr_209717.PDF (last page). OData lists the bill (BillID 148871) at sitting 74 under "for second-third reading" and "for third reading". Protocol: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/Plenum/1_ptm_250028.pdf . Checked: 14 October 2026.
- Reading: **single source** (OData only). The protocol text read does not name the reading for the final vote; it calls it a vote on the law. OData records the bill as laid on the table at sitting 41 (13 June 1949) and on the plenum agenda for first-reading debate at sittings 42 to 56 (14 June to 18 July 1949). The first-reading sittings were not read.
- Revenue chapter: **single source.** "בעד הצעת הוועדה — 55, נגד — 22", then "הפרק "הכנסה" של התקציב לשנת 1949/50 בנוסח ועדת־הכספים נתקבל" (printed page 1473).
- Expenditure chapter, total 40,178,000 pounds: **single source.** The page image reads "בעד הפרק "הוצאה" בנוסח ועדת־הכספים — 58, נגד — 25" (printed page 1506). The PDF's OCR text layer reads the "against" figure as 23; the text layer is a reading of the same scan, not a second record, and the image shows 25. The 40,178,000 total matches section 1(a) of the enacted law.
- The law as a whole: **single source.** After amendments to section 6 (defence budget) by Repetur and Bader were rejected, the chair, Yosef Sprinzak, put the law to the vote: "בעד החוק — 60, נגד — 25", then "חוק התקציב לשנת־הכספים 1949/50, תש"ט־1949 בנוסח ועדת־הכספים נתקבל" (printed page 1508). No abstentions are recorded.
- Fiscal year: **single source.** Section 1(a) of the enacted law covers 1 April 1949 to 31 March 1950 and includes the April-June 1949 and transitional (July 1949 and No. 2) budget laws (PDF page 1, text layer).

**1950/51 Budget Law: third-reading votes, 29 June 1950, sitting 159 of the First Knesset.**

- Date and sitting: **verified.** Protocol heading, sitting 159, 14 Tammuz 5710 (29 June 1950), "קריאה שניה וקריאה שלישית": https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/Plenum/1_ptm_250044.pdf . The enacted law's footnote reads "נתקבל בכנסת ביום י"ד בתמוז תש"י (29 ביוני 1950)" (text layer): https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/law/1_lsr_209714.PDF . OData lists the bill (BillID 148868) at sitting 159 under "for third reading". Checked: 14 October 2026. Section 1(a) of the same law covers 1 April 1950 to 31 March 1951 and includes the April-June 1950 budget law (text layer, **single source**).
- Expenditure budget: **single source.** The chair, Yosef Burg, first declared it adopted without a count; Yohanan Bader (Herut) asked for the votes to be counted again. On the second vote ("הצבעה שניה"): "בעד תקציב ההוצאה לשנת 1950/51 בנוסח הוועדה — 48, נגד — 24", "נתקבל" (printed page 1975). Eri Jabotinsky (Herut) said he had abstained on every item and would vote against the law as a whole (same page).
- The law as a whole: **single source.** After sections 1 to 10 were adopted and the Repetur, Bader and Rokach amendments to sections 4 to 6 rejected, Burg said "נצביע עכשיו על החוק כולו, כפי שהוצע על־ידי הוועדה": "בעד חוק התקציב לשנת 1950/51 בנוסח הוועדה — 44, נגד — 25", "חוק התקציב לשנת 1950/51 בנוסח הוועדה נתקבל" (printed page 1977, PDF page 24). The body does not label this vote by reading; the sitting summary at the end of the same PDF records the law "בקריאה שלישית נתקבל ... בנוסח ועדת־הכספים". The summary and body are one record, so the "third reading" label is single source.

**1951/52 Budget Law: second and third readings, 30 and 31 December 1951, sittings 36 and 37 of the Second Knesset.**

- Sittings and dates: **verified.** OData lists the bill (BillID 148862) at sitting 30 (11 December 1951, laid for first reading), sittings 28, 33 and 34 (on the agenda for first-reading debate), sitting 36 (30 December 1951, for second-third reading) and sitting 37 (31 December 1951, for second-third and third reading). The enacted law's footnote gives adoption on 31 December 1951 (see the next section). Sitting 36: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/Plenum/2_ptm_250630.pdf ; sitting 37: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/Plenum/2_ptm_250393.pdf . Both protocol headings read "קריאה שניה וקריאה שלישית". Sitting 37 opened at 16.10 on 31 December and closed at 5.20 the next morning; the law carries the sitting's date. Checked: 14 October 2026.
- Revenue chapter, 113,637,000 pounds, sitting 36: **single source.** "בעד הצעת הוועדה — 53, נגד — 51", "פרק ההכנסה של התקציב בנוסח הוועדה נתקבל" (printed page 826). This is the closest budget vote recovered so far. The sitting summary records only "הוחלט: לקבל את פרק ההכנסה של התקציב בנוסח הוועדה".
- Expenditure chapter, sitting 37: **single source.** "בעד פרק ההוצאה של התקציב — 51, נגד — 31" (printed page 877). Before it Yeshayahu Forder said the Progressive faction would vote against the budget law and the budget.
- The law as a whole, sitting 37: **single source.** After Sneh, Bader and Wilner amendments to sections 1, 4, 5, 6 and 9 were rejected: "בעד חוק התקציב — 50, נגד — 38", "חוק התקציב לשנת־הכספים 1951/52, בנוסח הוועדה, נתקבל" (printed page 878). The text does not label this vote by reading; Moshe Sneh's remark just before it, "הקריאה הזאת יכולה לבטל את הקריאה השניה", and the OData status point to the third reading, but no line in the protocol says so.
- Sitting 37 also recorded close votes on single items, for example Forder's proposal to cut every ministry by 3 per cent: a first vote "הקולות שקולים" (tied, no figures given), then 36 for and 47 against on the recount (PDF page 4). These are amendment votes, not budget votes.

**Not found.** Separate first-reading votes: the first-reading sittings for 1949/50 (sittings 42 to 56) and 1951/52 (sittings 28 to 34) were identified through OData but not read. Abstention counts: none of the five chapter or whole-law votes above records abstentions. Development-budget laws were not followed.

### The 1951/52 annual law

The 1951/52 Budget Law, section 1, defines 1 April 1951-31 March 1952 and includes amounts already authorised by April-September and October-December 1951 interim laws. Its footnote records adoption on 31 December 1951, under the Second Knesset. Section 4 gives the Finance Committee a defence-budget role on the government's proposal; section 5 distinguishes transfers needing committee approval from lower-level transfers. Source: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/law/2_lsr_595048.pdf . The annual law's existence and late adoption are verified from text; its passage margin is not.

### Second Knesset and the fiscal year

The 1953/54 Expenditure Budget Law section 1 explicitly defines 1 April 1953-31 March 1954. It incorporates expenditure already authorised by April-June and July-August interim budget laws. Its footnote records adoption on 18 August 1953. Sections 3-4 give the Finance Committee roles in defence allocations and specified transfers, rather than treating every budget change as a new full-plenum budget vote: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/law/2_lsr_560568.pdf .

The 1955/56 Budget Law section 1 explicitly defines 1 April 1955-31 March 1956. Section 4 gives defence-budget allocation to the Finance Committee on the government's proposal. Its footnote records adoption on 31 March 1955, the day before the fiscal year began: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/law/2_lsr_560767.pdf . Together with the 1951/52 law above, these laws prove April-March for three sampled years. The other annual laws must be read before treating the pattern as verified for every year in the slice.

Second Knesset sitting 218, 26 March 1953, records the Finance Committee chair, Israel Guri, presenting the 1952/53 supplementary budget (Budget Law (No. 2) for 1952/53, IL 4,465,000) in second/third readings, opposition amendments being rejected and the law with its schedule being adopted. The same sitting handles an April-June 1953 interim budget. This confirms supplementary/interim legislation was used; it does not prove a close final tally: https://fs.knesset.gov.il/2/Plenum/2_ptm_250434.pdf .

## Retrieval limits

The official PDF text was read through a readable fetch. Direct PDF downloads returned an HTML response in this environment, so original scanned-page pixels were not inspected. In review (11 October 2026) the PDFs downloaded directly; the scan images were checked for the 1948 tally (sitting 23, printed page 42), the 1950/51 sitting summary, the Transition Law's adoption footnote, Ordinance No. 46 and section 1 of the 1953/54 and 1955/56 laws. The Knesset budget-history page and the laws-as-enacted index below would not load from outside Israel, so they were not re-checked. On 14 October 2026 the plenum protocols for sittings 74 (1949), 159 (1950), 36 and 37 (1951) and the enacted 1949/50 and 1950/51 laws downloaded directly as PDFs with a text layer; every tally quoted above was checked against the page image, and one OCR misreading was found (1949/50 expenditure, 23 in the text layer, 25 on the scan). The DOC versions listed in OData for the same sittings (for example https://fs.knesset.gov.il/1/Plenum/1_ptm_254220.doc ) were downloaded but hold almost no text, so they were not used. OCR can corrupt digits, Hebrew years and margins. Before asserting an exact vote count, legal amount or adoption date as game data, inspect the source scan and corroborate it with another record.
