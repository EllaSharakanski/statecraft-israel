# 1977-93 events research: 6 June 1982 to 8 December 1987

**IN PROGRESS, not ready for review.** Started 24 October 2026 for issue #162. Research only:
nothing here changes `content/`, no event is drafted, and there are no proposed tags, options,
effects or card text. Every URL was opened on the date given beside it.

**Purpose.** A sourced list of the events of the window the issue sets, 6 June 1982 to 8 December
1987. For each: the date or dates, a short factual summary, the sources, and both values where
sources disagree. This fills the gap between `events-1977-82.md` (#1428, ends 5 June 1982) and
`events-1987-93.md` (#1427, starts 9 December 1987), and keeps their shape and conventions.

**Sensitive material.** Most of this window is sensitive: the September 1982 chain including Sabra
and Shatila, Bus 300, and the Jewish Underground. For those, official findings get their own
sourced lines, a casualty figure is given only with the source that states it and that source's
basis, and disagreement is recorded as disagreement with every version and no compromise number.
Nothing is said about intent beyond what a cited source states. How any of this is shown in the
game is the owner's call and nothing is proposed about it.

**Conventions** (as in `events-1952-56.md`, and as used in the two sibling notes)
- **[V]** verified: an authoritative primary source, or two independent sources that agree. The
  Knesset's own record is treated as primary.
- **[S]** single source, a page not opened in full, a day worked out from a bulletin date, or a
  fact resting only on Wikipedia. Wikipedia is a pointer, cited by `oldid`, and a fact carried only
  by it is [S].
- **[C]** sources conflict. Every version is given with its source, and none is picked.
- **[GAP]** not found, or the source could not be reached.
- **No quotes are invented.** Short phrases in quotation marks are copied from the cited page.
- **Private people are referred to by role, not by name** (the owner ruling applied across the
  sibling notes in #1492). Office holders are named. Victims, attackers, captives and convicted
  individuals are described by role even where the cited source names them, and their names are
  not reproduced here.
- **our reading** marks an interpretation and is not attributed to a source.

## The September 1982 chain

Sourced from the JTA archive, each item opened 24 October 2026. **The date on a JTA archive page is
the bulletin date**; "today" inside an item means its dispatch day, usually the day before, as the
sibling notes record. Dates below say which they are.

### C1. Bashir Gemayel killed, 14 September 1982 [S], **computed here**

JTA bulletin **16 September 1982**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/israel-troops-enter-west-beirut-following-gemayels-assassination:
Gemayel "succumbed to injuries sustained when a massive bomb explosion destroyed the head quarters
of his Christian Phalangist Party in east Beirut where he was attending a meeting". The same item:
"Eight other persons were reported dead and at least 50 injured", and it calls him "the 34-year-old
Phalangist leader".

The day is in the sources, and an earlier draft of this note wrongly called it a **[GAP]**. Three
items give it between them:

- 16 September bulletin: "Israeli troops and armor entered west Beirut in force **last night**
  after the death of President-elect Bashir Gemayel in a bomb blast **yesterday** was confirmed."
- 17 September bulletin,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/some-cabinet-members-critical-of-decision-to-order-israeli-troops-into-west-beirut:
  "the assassination of Lebanon's President-elect Bashir Gemayel **Tuesday**".
- 4 October bulletin,
  https://www.jta.org/archive/two-high-court-justices-and-retired-army-officer-to-comprise-commission-of-inquiry:
  "the decision for the Israeli army to enter west Beirut on **September 15**".

**Computed here:** 14 September 1982 was a Tuesday, so the blast is **14 September 1982** and the
entry into West Beirut **15 September 1982**. The 16 September bulletin's "yesterday" agrees, its
dispatch day being 15 September. [S], because this rests on the wire's wording rather than on a
record of the event.

### C2. The IDF enters West Beirut [S]

Same bulletin, **16 September 1982**. An IDF spokesman is quoted: "The IDF forces' entry was
accomplished without incident." The item then reports that "by mid-morning, reports from Beirut
said leftwing forces and Palestine Liberation Organization remnants left behind when the PLO
evacuated west Beirut last month had opened fire on Israeli troops", with "stiff fighting involving
tanks, bazooka, mortar and machinegun fire".

JTA bulletin **17 September 1982**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/some-cabinet-members-critical-of-decision-to-order-israeli-troops-into-west-beirut:
Begin "called an urgent special session of the Cabinet for later tonight". On the argument made for
the entry, the item reports what Begin and Sharon "are expected to argue": that forces "had to move
swiftly into west Beirut to prevent Moslem leftists and a residual PLO force there from stepping
into the vacuum created by the assassination and to prevent Gemayel's Christian Phalangists from
wreaking bloody vengeance for their leader's death". **our reading:** that is the JTA's account of
an argument expected to be made, not a finding, and it is recorded here as such.

### C3. The massacre at Sabra and Shatila — **[C]** on the dates, **[GAP]** on the toll

The three items read for this note give three different spans, and none is picked:

| Bulletin | What it says |
|---|---|
| 29 September | "the massacre of Palestinian civilians in the Shatila and Sabra refugee camps in west Beirut **September 16-17**" |
| 30 September ([link](https://www.jta.org/archive/begin-says-he-regards-himself-as-the-premier-as-fully-responsible-for-beirut-massacre)) | "The massacre of the Palestinians at two refugee camps in west Beirut **reportedly began the night of Thursday, September 16**" |
| 4 October | "the massacre of Palestinian civilians by units of the Lebanese Phalangist militia in west Beirut **September 16-18**" |

"Reportedly" is the 30 September item's own word and is kept. All three agree the 16th is the
start; they differ on the end, 17 or 18, and one gives no end at all. **[C]**, recorded as a
conflict between contemporaneous wire reports rather than resolved here.

**The toll is no longer a [GAP].** It is now taken from the Commission's own report, which is
reachable after all (see the routes table). The report does not give "a" figure, and this note does
not either: it sets out several, each with whose count it is, and the Commission's own warning
against precision. See C6 below.

### C4. Begin on his responsibility; the commission is decided [S]

JTA bulletin **30 September 1982**, same item. The responsibility is stated in the item's opening
sentence and is narrower than a bare "responsible for the massacre": "Premier Menachem Begin said
today he regarded himself, **as Prime Minister, as fully responsible for any negligence or
wrongdoing that might have happened in connection with the Beirut massacre**." Only then:
addressing the Knesset Foreign Affairs and Security Committee, Begin "said he accepted this
responsibility even though he had actually learned of the massacre only after it was over — on
Saturday afternoon, September 18", and the item adds in parentheses: "(Begin first heard of the
massacre in a BBC world service broadcast.)" He "firmly denied that he or Defense Minister Ariel
Sharon had sought to duck an inquiry."

JTA bulletin **29 September 1982**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/unanimous-cabinet-decision-a-judicial-commission-will-conduct-full-formal-inquiry-into-massacre-of:
a unanimous Cabinet decision for a full judicial inquiry. Cabinet secretary Dan Meridor's statement,
quoted verbatim: "The matter which will be suggested to inquiry is all the facts and persons
connected with the atrocity which was carried out by a unit of the Lebanese forces against the
civilian population in the Shatila and Sabra camps." The Cabinet gave as its reason that it acted
"in order to put an end to the false libels to the effect that the Israeli government has something
to hide in this matter or that it would like to avoid its full clarification". Both phrases are the
Cabinet's own words as the bulletin reports them, kept as written.

**The terms of reference are worded three ways across the two items, and the differences are not
cosmetic.** The 29 September bulletin carries both Meridor's statement, "all the facts and persons
connected with the atrocity", and a broader formulation he gave for the terms themselves, "all
facts and factors (i.e. institutions and individuals) related to the atrocity"; the 30 September
bulletin
(https://www.jta.org/archive/begin-says-he-regards-himself-as-the-premier-as-fully-responsible-for-beirut-massacre)
reports Begin's letter to Kahan as "all the facts and factors connected to the atrocity perpetrated
by a unit of the Lebanese forces". "Persons" and "factors (i.e. institutions and
individuals)" are not the same scope, so all three are recorded.

The same 29 September item also carries the Cabinet's own chronology, which opens: "On Tuesday,
September 21, the Cabinet resolved to hold a discussion on an appropriate method of examining the
acts regarding the atrocities carried out by a unit from the Lebanese forces in Beirut and to
report on its decisions. The Minister of Justice announced in the Knesset in the name of the
government that the decision would be adopted in the very near future. On Friday, September 24,
the Cabinet unanimously resolved to ask the President of the Supreme Court to examine the facts
connected with the above mentioned atrocity. The Cabinet is convinced that by employing this
authority it took the appropriate way to examine the tragic incident as swiftly as possible and
with complete objectivity." The statement runs on past that; the rest is not quoted here.

An earlier draft said the text was cut at "a uni"; that was an artefact of how this note first
extracted the page, not the page, and it is corrected. A later draft also wrote `[resolved]` in
brackets as though it were supplied; the page prints "unanimously resolved", so the brackets are
gone.

The same item also reports, of the Saturday night before: "an estimated 400,000 people held a rally
in Tel Aviv". The figure is the source's, with its own "estimated".

Commission asserted that Israel had indirect responsibility for the massacre".

### C7. The number killed, as the report itself puts it [V]

Read from **page 26 of 64** as a rendered image. The Commission's own first word on this is that
the question cannot be settled:

> "It is impossible to determine precisely the number of persons who were slaughtered. The numbers
> cited in this regard are to a large degree tendentious and are not based on an exact count by
> persons whose reliability can be counted on."

With that said, these are the figures the report gives, each kept with whose count it is:

| Count | Figure | What the report says about it |
|---|---|---|
| Red Cross representatives (letter of the head of the Red Cross delegation, exhibit 153) | **328 bodies** | "This figure, however, does not include all the bodies, since it is known that a number of families buried bodies on their own initiative without reporting their actions to the Red Cross." Bodies were also removed "in trucks", and more may lie "under the ruins in the camps or in the graves that were dug by the assailants near the camps". |
| Red Cross list of the missing | **359 persons** who disappeared in West Beirut between 18 August and 20 September | "with most of the missing having disappeared from Sabra and Shatilla in mid-September" |
| A document that reached the Commission (exhibit 151) | **460** bodies found from 18.9.82 to 30.9.82 | Counted by "the Lebanese Red Cross, the International Red Cross, the Lebanese Civil Defense, the medical corps of the Lebanese army, and by relatives of the victims". Of the 460, "**109 Lebanese and 328 Palestinians**, along with Iranians, Syrians and members of other nationalities". |
| Palestinian sources | "a far greater number of persons killed, sometimes even of thousands" | The report declines both this and the Lebanese document: "we can rely neither on the numbers appearing in the document from Lebanese sources, nor on the numbers originating in Palestinian sources." |
| I.D.F. intelligence (testimony of the director of Military Intelligence, pp. 139-140) | **between 700 and 800** | "This may well be the number most closely corresponding with reality." |

On who the dead were, from the itemization of the list of 460: "the great majority of the dead were
males; as for women and children, there were **8 Lebanese women and 12 Lebanese children, and 7
Palestinian women and 8 Palestinian children**."

The Commission's own conclusion on scale: "Taking into account the fact that Red Cross personnel
counted no more that 328 bodies, it would appear that the number of victims of the massacre was not
as high as a thousand, and certainly not thousands." **"no more that"** is the page's own wording,
a "that" where "than" is meant, and it is not corrected here.

The report also notes a difficulty that bears on every figure above: "it is difficult to distinguish
between victims of combat operations and victims of acts of slaughter. We cannot rule out the
possibility that various reports included also victims of combat operations from the period
antedating the assassination of Bashir."

**No single number is given in this note.** The report itself gives none, declines two of the counts
it reports, and says of a third only that it "may well be" closest. Collapsing that into one figure
would misrepresent the source.

### C9. The Commission's recommendations [V]

Read from **pages 61 and 62 of 64** as rendered images. The square brackets below are the
translation's own, not mine.

The section opens with the Commission explaining why it recommends anything at all:

> "With regard to the following recommendations concerning a group of men who hold senior positions
> in the Government and the Israel Defense Forces, we have taken into account [the fact] that each
> one of these men has to his credit [the performance of] many public or military services rendered
> with sacrifice and devotion on behalf of the State of Israel. If nevertheless we have reached the
> conclusion that it is incumbent upon us to recommend certain measures against some of these men,
> it is out of the recognition that the gravity of the matter and its implications for the
> underpinnings of public morality in the State of Israel call for such measures."

Everyone named below is named in the report by office; all are public office holders.

| Named | What the Commission recommended |
|---|---|
| The Prime Minister, the Foreign Minister, and the Head of the Mossad | "it is sufficient to determine responsibility and there is no need for any further recommendations." |
| G.O.C. Northern Command Major General Amir Drori | "He took certain measures for terminating the Phalangists' actions, and his guilt lies in that he did not continue with these actions." It is "sufficient to determine the responsibility of Major General Drori without recourse to any further recommendation." |
| The Minister of Defense, Mr. Ariel Sharon | "the Minister of Defense bears personal responsibility." It is "fitting that the Minister of Defense draw the appropriate personal conclusions arising out of the defects revealed with regard to the manner in which he discharged the duties of his office - and if necessary, that the Prime Minister consider whether he should exercise his authority under Section 21-A(a) of the Basic Law: the Government", under which "the Prime Minister may, after informing the Cabinet of his intention to do so, remove a minister from office." |
| The Chief of Staff, Lt.-Gen. Rafael Eitan | "We have arrived at grave conclusions with regard to the acts and omissions of the Chief of Staff". Because he "is about to complete his term of service in April, 1983" and an extension "is not under consideration", there is "no [practical] significance to a recommendation with regard to his continuing in office", so it is "sufficient to determine responsibility without making any further recommendation." |
| The Director of Military Intelligence, Major General Yehoshua Saguy | "the various extremely serious omissions" of his office. "We recommend that Major General Yehoshua Saguy not continue as Director of Military Intelligence." |
| Division Commander Brigadier General Amos Yaron | "we recommend that Brigadier General Amos Yaron not serve in the capacity of a field commander in the Israel Defense Forces, and that this recommendation not be reconsidered before three years have passed." |

Beyond individuals, the Commission recommended "that, in addition to internal comptrol in this
matter, an investigation into the shortcomings and the manner of correcting them be undertaken by
an expert or experts, to be appointed by a Ministerial Defense Committee." ("comptrol" is the
page's spelling.)

**Note the asymmetry, since it is easy to lose:** only two of the six entries carry an operative
recommendation (Saguy and Yaron). For the Prime Minister, the Foreign Minister, the Head of the
Mossad, Drori and Eitan the Commission said determining responsibility was enough. For Sharon it
recommended that he "draw the appropriate personal conclusions", and raised the Prime Minister's
removal power only as something to "consider" "if necessary". Nothing here says what then happened;
that is the implementation, and it is not written yet.

### C9a. How the wire reported the recommendations, and where it differs from the report [C]

JTA bulletin **9 February 1983**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/commission-of-inquiry-makes-public-its-report-recommends-sharon-resign-or-be-dismissed-by-begin-fau.
The report was "published here **this morning**", which on a 9 February bulletin puts publication
on **8 February 1983** (**computed here**) and agrees with the date printed on the report itself
(C6). Two independent sources on the date, so [V] for the publication day.

**On Sharon the wire is stronger than the report.** The bulletin says the commission "called for
the resignation of Defense Minister Ariel Sharon **or his dismissal** by Premier Menachem Begin".
The report's own words (C9, read from page 62) are that it is "fitting that the Minister of Defense
draw the appropriate personal conclusions", and that "**if necessary**" the Prime Minister
"**consider** whether he should exercise his authority" to remove a minister. Those are not the
same thing. **The report is the primary source and is what this note relies on**; the wire's
framing is recorded here because it is how the recommendation was reported at the time, and the
difference between the two is itself a fact about the episode.

Other points from the same bulletin, which the report text read so far does not contradict:

- On Begin, it "accepted Begin's testimony that he knew nothing of the massacres until he heard of
  them from a foreign radio broadcast after the fact. But it faulted the Premier for his detachment
  from events, though it found mitigating circumstances."
- On Shamir, it was "sharply critical of Foreign Minister Yitzhak Shamir for disregarding
  information from a Cabinet colleague that the massacres were occurring **but did not call for his
  resignation**." That matches C9, where the Prime Minister, Foreign Minister and Head of the
  Mossad draw no further recommendation.
- On Eitan, his "dismissal it would have asked for but for the fact that Eitan's term of office
  expires in April and an extension of his command is not under consideration" — the same reasoning
  the report gives in C9.
- The reason given for Sharon's responsibility: "for willfully ignoring the obvious dangers of
  'vengeance and bloodshed' against civilians when he allowed armed Christian Phalangists to enter
  the west Beirut camps **last September 16**". Note this bulletin dates the entry of the
  Phalangists to 16 September, which bears on the C3 date conflict without settling it.

**A spelling difference:** the wire has "Gen. Yehoshua **Seguy**"; the report has "Major General
Yehoshua **Saguy**". Both are reproduced as their sources print them and neither is corrected.

### C10. What followed the report [S]

JTA bulletin **24 February 1983**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/knesset-confirms-arens-as-defense-minister-report-that-meir-rosenne-a-veteran-diplomat-will-repla:
"The Knesset voted **61-51** today to approve the appointment of **Moshe Arens** as Minister of
Defense, succeeding **Ariel Sharon who was forced to resign**." **Computed here:** the vote is
**23 February 1983**, the dispatch day.

The same item records what did *not* change: Labor Alignment MK Haim Barlev "warned that the Likud
government would live to regret its decision **to allow Sharon to remain in the Cabinet and on two
key committees — the Ministerial Defense Committee and the Steering Committee for negotiations
with Lebanon**." Barlev, quoted: "When Arens enters the Cabinet and the Defense Ministry, he will
have Sharon breathing down his neck on one side and from the other side a Premier who came out as
he did from the Kahan report."

**Read against C9 this is the shape of the implementation**: the Commission recommended that the
Minister of Defense "draw the appropriate personal conclusions", and raised removal only as
something for the Prime Minister to "consider" "if necessary". The wire says he "was forced to
resign" the defence ministry and stayed in the Cabinet. Whether that satisfied the recommendation
is a judgement this note does not make.

What became of the other recommendations, on Saguy and Yaron, is **not sourced yet**.

## Begin's resignation

### D1. The announcement, 28 August 1983 [S], **computed here**

JTA bulletin **31 August 1983** (a Wednesday),
https://www.jta.org/archive/begins-decision-to-resign-is-final-but-postpones-submitting-resignation-letter-to-the-president:
"Begin's final decision today ended two days of intensive consultations and speculation about the
Premier's intention to resign. The Premier first announced his move at the end of a routine Cabinet
session Sunday, catching the ministers and the nation by surprise." Taking the dispatch day as 30
August (Tuesday), the Sunday before it is **28 August 1983**; 28 August 1983 was a Sunday.

An earlier draft of this note called this a **[C]** against the 15 September bulletin's "just 19
days after", and **that was wrong**. That item opens "Menachem Begin offically resigned as Prime
Minister **Thursday**" (the misspelling is the page's), and 15 September 1983 was a Thursday, so
the formal act is the 15th, not the 14th. From Sunday 28 August to Thursday 15 September is 18 days
exclusive and **19 counting both ends**, which is what "just 19 days after" describes. The two
bulletins agree; there is no conflict, and the tag is dropped.

### D2. The formal resignation, 15 September 1983 [S]

JTA bulletin **15 September 1983**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/begin-officially-resigns-hands-in-his-resignation-to-herzog, opening:
"Menachem Begin offically resigned as Prime Minister Thursday. **His formal letter of resignation
was delivered to President Chaim Herzog by Cabinet Secretary Dan Meridor.**" That settles what the
headline ("Hands in His Resignation to Herzog") leaves open: the letter went by the Cabinet
Secretary, not in person. The item gives the reason: "A spokesman for Begin told reporters that the
70-year-old Israeli leader has a facial rash and, being unable to shave, did not want to appear
before the President. But speculation was rife that Begin, who has not left his home for over a
week, is seriously ill."

The same item: Likud "elected Foreign Minister Yitzhak Shamir to replace him as party leader on
September I". The page reads **"September I"**, a capital I where a 1 is meant; the date is **not**
corrected here, only flagged. 1 September 1983 was a Thursday.

## The bank shares crisis, October 1983

### E1. The money market [S], with a figure the page garbles

JTA bulletin **11 October 1983**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/near-panic-dominates-israels-money-market: "The commercial banks
unexpectedly announced today that the Shekel will sell at the rate of 70-99 to the U.S. Dollar,
down from the 65.63 rate that prevailed last Friday. Two hours later, the Bank of Israel, the
country's central bank, announced an official base rate of 69.27".

**The page reads "70-99".** Next to 65.63 and 69.27 it is almost certainly 70.99 with the decimal
point lost, but it is **not corrected here**: the figure is quoted as printed and marked. A rate is
not a thing to tidy silently.

Same item: "Speculators who had disregarded the Finance Ministry's promise to maintain the Shekel
and purchased Dollars, earned a profit of over five percent. Those who trusted Finance Minister
Yoram Aridor's assurances that devaluation would be gradual, registered losses of between 15-30
percent."

### E2. The decision to support the bank shares [S]

https://www.jta.org/archive/dispute-in-cabinet-over-its-earlier-decision-to-shore-up-sagging-commercial-bank-shares:

### E3. Aridor resigns [S], and it cross-checks the Knesset record [V]

## Bus 300, April 1984

The handling here is the one set out in the claim: the official version gets its own line, the
reports that contradict it get theirs, and neither is presented as settled.

### G1. The hijacking and the storming [S]

JTA bulletin **26 April 1984**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/two-mks-demand-probe-into-the-killing-of-four-terrorists-who-hijacked-a-telaviv-ashkelon-bus:
"According to the army, two of the terrorists were killed instantly when troops stormed the bus in
the early hours of **Friday, April 13**". 13 April 1984 was a Friday, which agrees.

JTA bulletin **14 May 1984**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/army-report-due-on-its-probe-of-death-of-four-arab-terrorists, places
it "in the Gaza Strip April 13". Both items are about a bus hijacked on the Tel Aviv to Ashkelon
route, as the first one's headline says.

### G2. What happened to the men taken alive — **[C]**

The two accounts are set against each other in the sources themselves, and both are given here.

| | |
|---|---|
| The official version | 14 May bulletin: "two terrorists were killed instantly and the other two died shortly afterwards of their wounds". |
| What reporters at the scene said | Same bulletin: "newsmen at the scene reported that at least one of the terrorists was taken into custody unharmed, implying that he was killed after his capture". |

The 26 April bulletin frames the same gap as a suspicion to be cleared: MKs Ehud Olmert (Likud) and
Victor Shemtov (Labor Alignment) "agreed today that the facts must be ascertained to relieve Israel
of suspicion, implicit in foreign media reports, that one of the terrorists was murdered after his
capture". Defence Minister Moshe Arens "said in a radio interview Monday that he had no reason to
disbelieve the army's version of what occurred", adding that the military was "routinely" examining
the incident.

**No conclusion is drawn here.** What the inquiries found belongs to G3 and is not yet sourced.

### G3a. The pardons, 1986 — part of the gap, filled from a 1987 item [S]

JTA bulletin **17 April 1987**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/shin-bet-appears-to-be-involved-in-a-new-scandal. The bulletin is
mainly about a different matter (see G5), but in passing it supplies a piece of the Bus 300
sequence that G3 and G4 leave open.

It describes a former senior Shin Bet operative as "**one of three who received a Presidential
pardon last year** in connection with the murder of two captured Arab bus hijackers by Shin Bet
agents in 1984", and adds: "**None of the three was ever formally charged or tried**."

Two things follow, and the note states them as the source does and no further:

- By 1987 the wire describes the deaths flatly as "**the murder of two captured** Arab bus
  hijackers by Shin Bet agents", with no hedge. That is the same shift already noted at G4 against
  the 1984 official version in G2.
- Three people were pardoned "last year", so during 1986, **without charge or trial**. The exact
  date of the pardons is **not in this item** and is not inferred from "last year".

The operatives are referred to here by role, under the owner ruling; the bulletin names one of
them.

**What is still missing between G3 and G4** is the Zorea commission's findings and the Landau
inquiry, which #162 names. Neither is sourced yet.

### G5. A second Shin Bet matter, April 1987 [S]

Same bulletin. It is not on #162's list and is recorded only because it sits inside the window and

"The Shin Bet ... appears to be implicated in a new scandal involving the possible fabrication of
evidence that sent an Israel Defense Force officer to prison for treason six years ago." The

### G4. The Shin Bet affair: the Harish committee, December 1986 [S]

JTA bulletin **30 December 1986**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/shamir-absolved-of-any-wrongdoing-in-1984-arab-bus-hijackers-killings.

## The Jewish Underground, April 1984

**On the name.** "Jewish Underground" is the heading #162 uses, and it is kept here for that

### H1. The bombs on the East Jerusalem buses [S]

https://www.jta.org/archive/attempted-mass-sabotage-of-arab-owned-buses-shocks-israelis: "The

**Computed here:** the Friday before a 30 April 1984 bulletin (a Monday, dispatch day 29 April) is

On the arrests, the same item: "As of noon today, at least **30 suspects** had been rounded up and
remanded in custody for 15 days by district court judges all over the country. Under the law, none

### H2. The trial and the sentences, 22 July 1985 [S]

JTA bulletin **23 July 1985**,
https://www.jta.org/archive/three-jewish-underground-members-sentenced-to-life-imprisonment-twelve-other-members-sentenced-from.
**Computed here:** "today" on a 23 July bulletin puts the sentencing on **22 July 1985**.

**The bulletin names the convicted and I do not reproduce the names**, under the owner ruling: they
are private individuals. They are described by role below.

- "Three convicted members of a Jewish terrorist underground received mandatory life sentences for
  murder today." The three given life were **the underground's leader and two other members**. They
  "were found guilty of murder for their part in a machinegun and grenade attack on the Islamic
  College in Hebron in which **three Palestinian students were killed**." (The students are not
  named in the bulletin text read.)
- "Twelve others drew sentences ranging from **four months to seven years** imprisonment on a
  variety of lesser counts, all involving acts of violence against Arab residents of the West Bank
  and conspiracy to blow up Islamic shrines in East Jerusalem."
- The sentences were "pronounced by a three-judge panel in Jerusalem District Court" and "marked
  the end of the most controversial and politically explosive trial in Israel's history". The
  wire's characterisation is kept as the wire's.
- On the scale of the case: "There were originally **27 defendants** when the trial began over a
  year ago. **Ten** were sentenced in the course of the legal process as a result of
  plea-bargaining with the State Prosecutor. **Two**, who are Israel Defense Force officers, will
  be tried separately and are free on bail."
- The bulletin also records, without this note adopting it: the sentences "triggered new demands
  for pardon from rightwing politicians and nationalist and religious elements", and "All of the
  defendants are Orthodox Jews."

**Arithmetic worth flagging rather than resolving:** 3 life + 12 lesser = 15 sentenced on the day,
against "originally 27 defendants", of whom 10 were sentenced earlier by plea bargain and 2 are to
be tried separately. 15 + 10 + 2 = 27, which is consistent. Recorded because the counts could
easily be read as overlapping.

Clemency and later releases appear in other archive items (one headline has a President commuting
sentences) and are **not read yet**.

## The London Agreement, April 1987 — **[GAP]**, and why

Searched the JTA archive on 24 October 2026 for contemporaneous coverage of a Peres–Hussein
understanding reached in London in April 1987. **Nothing contemporaneous was found.** The hits are
later pieces looking back on secret contacts, and none of them was read for this note.

That absence is consistent with the agreement having been secret at the time, so it is not evidence
that nothing happened, and it is **not** written up from the later retrospectives. A source of a
different kind is needed: a published text of the understanding, a memoir, or a Knesset record of
the debate that followed. **[GAP]** until then.
