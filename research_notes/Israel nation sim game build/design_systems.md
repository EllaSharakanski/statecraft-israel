# Game-Design Systems of Nation/Government Sims and 4X Games: Lessons for an Israel 1880s-Today Government Sim

Research date: 2026-10-09. Tool budget limited (~19 calls). Several pages (Forbes, Paradox forum) returned 403/500, so some findings come from search-result summaries of those pages. Items marked "unverified" in Inferences/Gaps come from background knowledge and have no source here.

## 1. Civilization VII (2025): Ages, crises, legacy paths, leaders vs civs, and how it was received

### Takeaway
Civ VII splits one campaign into three self-contained "Ages" (Antiquity / Exploration / Modern). Each Age has a progress meter, four Legacy Paths, an end-of-age Crisis with negative "Crisis Policies," and a transition where most things reset and the player picks a new civ. Players did not like the abrupt resets or the forced civ switching, and Steam reviews ran about 48-51% positive. Firaxis spent the May 2026 "Test of Time" update (1.4) undoing much of this: one civ can now last the whole game, and Legacy Paths became looser "Triumphs." For a historically anchored nation sim, the lesson is to use eras as chapters but keep continuity of identity.

### Cited Findings
- **Structure:** there are three Ages, Antiquity, Exploration and Modern, fewer era divisions than earlier entries. Crisis events "simulate societal disruption" near the end of Antiquity and Exploration. At an age end, players choose a new civ (originally mandatory, later optional). Leaders and civs are picked independently, and "leaders" now include non-heads-of-state such as philosophers and scientists. — [Wikipedia: Civilization VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VII)
- **Ed Beach's framing:** "Ages represent a significant gameplay evolution for Civilization – one as big as when we moved from squares to hexes." Ages are meant as distinct playable chapters, with civs tied to their age so choices stay historically relevant. — [CivFanatics: Civ VII Ages](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/)
- **What resets at a transition:** Tech and civic trees reset to new trees. Legacy Paths reset. Most buildings are age-specific, and obsolete ones yield less and can be overbuilt. Obsolete policy cards are removed. Resources change. Most units cannot be built in later ages. CivFanatics said some cities may become towns but marked this unconfirmed.
- **What persists:** the leader (who grows via Attribute Points), "Ageless" buildings, wonders and unique quarters, commanders' XP, and Traditions, which stay slottable in any age. — [CivFanatics](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/)
- **Legacy Paths:** four per age (Science, Culture, Military, Economic), each with up to three milestones. Milestones reward Legacy Points, Attribute Points, Age Progress or an immediate benefit. Completing a path unlocks a "Golden Age" option for the next age. Making no progress unlocks a "Dark Age" legacy, a consolation bonus for failure. — [CivFanatics](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/); [2K Civ VII Glossary](https://civilization.2k.com/en-GB/civ-vii/glossary/)
- **Age Progress meter:** it ticks up every turn and gets boosts from milestones and from finishing the tech and civic trees. Narrative events fire at progress thresholds and unlock Crisis Policy slots. Crisis Policies are negative policies placed in their own slot set, and every civ must fill them. The Crisis ends in a scripted climax, for example a massive barbarian assault in Antiquity. All players transition at the same time. — [CivFanatics](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/); [2K Glossary](https://civilization.2k.com/en-GB/civ-vii/glossary/); [CivFanatics forum: Ages](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/gameplay-mechanic-discussion-ages.691392)
- **Example crisis rule (Revolts, Antiquity):** the revolt threshold becomes -1 Happiness, and any unhappy settlement damages one building or improvement per turn until its happiness recovers. — [Civ Wiki: Revolts](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Revolts_(Civ7))
- **Reception:**
  - Metacritic 79, and 77% of critics on OpenCritic recommend it. Critics criticized the UI "with little explanation," oversimplified mechanics, the ages system and civ switching, a tech tree that ends around the 1960s, and reliance on DLC.
  - Steam was "Mixed," with 48% recommending months after launch; most complaints targeted the abrupt transitions and civ switching.
  - Launch peak was 80k+ concurrent players. Civ V and Civ VI overtook it in active players in April 2025, and it fell below 5k concurrent players in May 2025.
  - Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick called initial sales "slow" in August 2025.
  - Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VII)
- **Patches and updates:**
  - Feb 2025, patch 1.0.1: UI and tooltip fixes.
  - Feb 2026, patch 1.3.2: UI, AI and performance.
  - May 2026, v1.4 "Test of Time": one civilization across the whole campaign.
  - Through July 2026: three DLC collections adding nine civs.
  - Announced for 2027: an "Arc of Tomorrow" Atomic Age to 2050, plus an *Earthrise* space expansion.
  - Source: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VII)
- **Test of Time contents (May 19, 2026):** you can "start and stay as any civ in any Age." Legacy Paths are replaced with "Triumphs," described as "a more flexible, sandbox-driven system." Victories are "totally reworked." New games are recommended, and a legacy beta branch is kept for old saves. — [Firaxis patch notes, May 19 2026](https://support.civilization.com/hc/en-us/articles/51685242291219-Civilization-VII-Patch-Notes-May-19-2026)
- **Post-update reception (Forbes, May 31 2026, via search summary because the page returned 403):** Triumphs are minor and major mini-challenges that can be ticked off during play, and each of the four victory types gets a score so progress is visible across Ages. In the 11 days after the update, no single day had more negative than positive Steam reviews, averaging 55%, against 51% lifetime. — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/barrycollins/2026/05/31/civilization-vii-finally-passes-test-of-time-as-review-scores-rise/)
- **Player sentiment (single forum posts, anecdotal):** Legacy Paths were "awfully restrictive," Crisis events "boring and meaningless," and DLC pricing too high. — [CivFanatics "Civ 7 in 2026"](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/civ-7-in-2026.701129/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1295660/discussions/0/690870479611758979/)

### Inferences
- **Eras work as content containers, not as identity resets.** For Israel, the eras are natural chapters: Ottoman Yishuv 1882-1917, Mandate 1917-48, early state 1948-67, 1967-77, 1977-92, Oslo / second intifada, and the 2000s-today. Each era can have its own policy cards, buildings, ministries and crises. The polity's continuity (institutions, demographics, debts, grievances) must carry over, because Civ VII's evidence is that players hate losing what they built.
- **The Crisis Policies mechanic transfers directly.** Era-ending crises are like the 1929 riots, the 1936-39 revolt, the 1947-49 war, the 1973 war, 1980s hyperinflation, the intifadas and October 7. Each could force "negative policy" slots such as rationing (Tzena), emergency regulations, or reserve mobilization. They must be meaningful and tied to how well the player prepared, or they will be read as "boring and meaningless."
- **The Golden Age / Dark Age legacy design suits a historical sim.** Failing an era's goals should produce a distinct, playable consequence rather than a game over.
- **Firaxis's reversal points toward Triumph-style optional goals over fixed paths.** Optional mini-goals plus visible scores across eras worked better than rigid paths.

- The Forbes page could not be fetched directly, so its numbers come from a search summary.
- I could not verify Civ VII's Steam rating as of October 2026.

## 2. Democracy 4 (Positech): the neural-network policy and voter simulation, UI and data files

### Takeaway
Democracy is the clearest model for a multi-ministry policy sim. Everything (policies, situations, simulation values, voter groups, dilemmas, events) is a node in a "neural network" of effects defined as equations in CSV files, with per-effect inertia (delay). Voters are blends of voter groups. The UI is the network itself: an icon web where hovering shows the causal links. Because it is fully data-driven, it is highly moddable and the authors can tune it.

### Cited Findings
- **The network:** "The simulation is based on a neural network." Voters, voter groups, policies and events are all "objects" linked by "effects." None are hard-coded; all load from CSV files.
- **Effect format:** each effect is target, value formula, optional operator and value, then inertia. In a policy formula, `x` is replaced by the slider's current value.
- **Inertia:** it averages the effect over recent slider values. For example, inertia 4 means a change takes up to 4 turns to fully apply.
- **Implementation and ministers:** a policy's effect is reduced if the policy is not yet fully implemented, and it is scaled by the competence of the relevant minister.
- **Data layout:**
  - `data/simulation/*.csv` holds the main data, such as `policies.csv`.
  - `prereqs.txt` holds per-country binary prerequisites such as `_prereq_has_coast`.
  - Translations live in a separate folder.
  - Mods go in `Documents\My Games\Democracy 4\mods` and Steam Workshop.
  - Sources: [Positech: Democracy 4 Modding](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/modding.html), [Policies](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/mod_policies.html)
- **Policies.csv:** rows starting with `#` are policies. Columns include an internal Name, a Slider (linked to sliders.csv for named positions), "Opposites" (mutually exclusive policies that each list the other, so implementing one auto-cancels the other), min/max cost and income with a function (default linear `0+(1.0*x)`), and effects in the syntax `[target],[value1][op1]([value2][op2][value3])[op3][value4],[inertia]`. Effects can multiply by other variables, for example adult education scaled by technology level. — [Positech: Modding Policies](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/mod_policies.html)
- **Chained effects:** Cliff Harris warns that changing one equation can "affect dozens, maybe hundreds of other policies, situations." He later extended equations to 5 and then 6 variables. — [Positech modding pages via search summary](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/mod_policies.html); [Cliff's blog: state employee/unemployment accuracy, May 2021](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2021/05/01/improving-accuracy-of-state-employee-unemployment-effects-in-democracy-4/)
- **Situations:**
  - These are emergent states such as crises or booms, defined in `situations.csv`.
  - Columns: Name, Department (where it shows on the main screen), PreReqs, an SVG Icon, Positive (green or red background), start and stop triggers "set with a similar gap", which is hysteresis, cost and income per turn with functions, then Inputs (before a `#`) and Effects (after it).
  - A `default` object can set a base level.
  - Source: [Positech: Modding Situations](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/mod_situations.html)
- **Voter model (as described on the Democracy 3 store page):** a custom neural network models individual voters, each a mixture of a subset of 21 voter groups (e.g., "a young, wealthy, liberal socialist commuter"). Group identification is variable and policies can shift it over time. Each voter's income, complacency and cynicism are modeled. Cascades are like trade policy affecting GDP, then unemployment, poverty, crime, tourism and back to GDP. — [Steam: Democracy 3](https://store.steampowered.com/app/245470)
- **Developer:** Positech is Cliff Harris's indie studio, making games since 1997. — [Steam: Democracy 3](https://store.steampowered.com/app/245470)

### Inferences
- **Use this architecture for the ministries layer.** Each ministry (education, health, infrastructure, legal, security, tourism, foreign relations, economy, immigration/aliyah) becomes a "department" cluster of policy sliders and situations on one shared variable graph, defined as data, which lets historians and modders edit it.
- **Inertia is the clean way to do "decisions with delayed consequences."** Education spending could take 20+ turns to affect GDP, while security operations act in 1-2 turns.
- **Hysteresis triggers stop flicker.** Situations like "Mass Unemployment," "Housing Crisis (ma'abarot)" or "Brain Drain" should use different start and stop thresholds.
- **The minister-competence multiplier maps well to coalition politics.** Coalition partners demand ministries, and a weak minister lowers that ministry's effectiveness.
- **Voter groups need to be Israel-specific and overlapping.** Examples are Ashkenazi/Mizrahi, Haredi, national-religious, secular, Arab citizens, olim from the FSU or Ethiopia, kibbutzniks, settlers, reservists, Tel Aviv middle class and the periphery. Voters are blends, so a "Mizrahi, religious-traditional, periphery, reservist" voter shows cross-pressure.
- **Before 1948 the "voters" are different.** The Yishuv period needs other legitimacy constituencies: Zionist Congress factions, the Histadrut, Ottoman and British authorities, philanthropists like Rothschild, and the Old Yishuv. The same graph can swap "approval" for "legitimacy/support" per era.

- I did not get exact Democracy 4 voter-group counts or the list of simulation values; the 21 groups are from the Democracy 3 page.
- Dilemma and event file formats (`mod_dilemmas.html`, `mod_events.html`) were not fetched.

## 3. Suzerain and Kingdom of Rizia: a narrative-driven presidency

### Takeaway
Suzerain is a text-heavy political RPG built on authored branching plus heavy state tracking. Its state-tracking tooling (articy:draft) is the backbone of its delayed consequences. The Rizia DLC adds resource layers (energy, authority) and territorial reclamation by diplomacy or force. It is the best reference for presenting the player as a person making decisions under pressure, as opposed to Democracy's systemic dashboard.

### Cited Findings
- **Tooling:** Torpor Games needed a scripting tool to "handle the massive amount of text and miriad of choices and consequences," and used articy:draft to iterate on story changes faster. — [articy case study](https://www.articy.com/en/?p=4725)
- **Design intent:** lead developer Ata Sergey Nowak discusses designing a political RPG that "forces players to commit to their actions" and aims to teach empathy. — [AV Club interview](https://www.avclub.com/suzerain-interview)
- **Studio skill requirements:** a Torpor job listing for a new project asks for experience with "decision-based narratives with consequences, state tracking, and reactivity." — [Work With Indies: Torpor Narrative Designer](https://workwithindies.com/careers/torpor-games-narrative-designer-m-f-d)
- **Kingdom of Rizia:** you play King Romus. It adds energy and authority resources, reclaiming lost territory "through diplomacy or force," and stability of the realm and of House Toras. The "Sovereign 3.1" update added more governance authority, changing advisor and foreign-power relationships, and a dozen new scenes. — [Epic Store: Kingdom of Rizia](https://store.epicgames.com/p/suzerain--kingdom-of-rizia-dlc); [GamingTrend: Sovereign 3.1](https://gamingtrend.com/news/suzerain-receives-new-sovereign-update-to-3-1/)
- **Reviews:** GamingTrend gave it 85/100 and called it a "compelling political drama/simulation" that demands patience with long text blocks and "often punishing decision-making." — [GamingTrend review](https://gamingtrend.com/feature/reviews/youve-got-my-vote-suzerain-review)

### Inferences
- **Suzerain-style authored scenes suit history's turning points.** Examples: Ben-Gurion and the Altalena, the reparations agreement debate (1952), the Lavon affair, the 1967 waiting period, the Camp David decision, and the Oslo handshake. These scenes would sit on top of a Democracy-style simulation.
- **The decision-to-consequence links need the scene layer and simulation layer to share one state store.** Suzerain's reliance on articy-style state tracking points the same way.
- **Long text is the known cost.** Keep authored scenes for the 1-3 pivotal decisions per era.

### Gaps
- I found no detailed source on how Suzerain's constitutional and legislative mechanics work (court appointments, constitutional amendment votes, the parliamentary vote-count UI). Background knowledge, unverified here: bills pass by counting supportive factions in the assembly, and a budget/economy screen tracks deficits.
- I found no detail on whether Rizia has a map or strategy layer beyond "reclaim territory."
- I found no sales data or GDC talk.

## 4. Hearts of Iron IV and Victoria 3 (Paradox): scripting, focus trees, historical vs alternate paths, pops and diplomacy

### Takeaway
Paradox has two models. HoI4 uses national focus trees with a "historical focus" AI toggle, which railroads the AI but leaves the player free. Victoria 3 moved scripted content away from random country-specific event popups toward "Journal Entries," which are generic, condition-gated objectives that can fire for any country meeting the triggers. Vic3 pairs these with a pop and interest-group "society sim." The trade-off is that sandbox-generic content feels historically thin to some players, while HoI4-style trees feel railroaded.

### Cited Findings
- **Victoria 3's framing:** Dev Diary 1 calls it a "Society Sim," a game about "the internal workings of the 19th-century country." Politics, economy and diplomacy are the three pillars, and "wars are a part of the game but it is not a wargame." It uses pops. — [NME](https://www.nme.com/news/long-awaited-sequel-victoria-3-announced-by-paradox-interactive-2946220) (via search summary)
- **Journal Entries (Dev Diary 31):** "compared to Victoria 2 the team shifted the focus of scripted content away from random event popups and towards the Journal system." The goals are to avoid "arbitrary historical events that occur for specific countries even when they don't make sense in relation to what's happening in the game," and to avoid content that is invisible because it fires only under hyper-specific conditions.
- **How entries work:** entries usually fire an event on activation that presents the situation "both narratively and mechanically." Availability is gated by triggers. For example, Confederate Canada requires researching Nationalism and is available to any country with Canadian subjects, not only Britain. — [Paradox forum DD31](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-31-journal-entries.1508059) (via search summary; direct fetch failed with 500)
- **Player criticism of the sandbox approach (anecdotal Steam posts):** "there is no historical event system in this game save for the journal… you didn't get the US civil war, you got a random radicalization." Another player notes WWI fires only if the Archduke is assassinated by Balkan separatists. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/529340/discussions/0/5675129542132329045)
- **Interests (Dev Diary 58):** "Interests" in strategic regions were made required for all diplomacy, including trade, after playtesting showed they felt insignificant. This made them "a far more central mechanic." — [Paradox DD58](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/dev-diary-58-interest-revisions)
- **Readability:**
  - Vic3 adopted CK3's nested tooltip system so "you don't need an econ degree." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/victoria-3/nested-tooltip-system)
  - A reviewer criticized notification popups piling up in the corner until ignored, and blocked diplomatic actions without explanation. — [GameCritics review](https://gamecritics.com/mitch-zehe/victoria-3-review/)
  - Destructoid praised the tutorial. — [Destructoid](https://destructoid.com/reviews/review-victoria-3-pc-paradox-interactive-strategy)
  - Metacritic 83. — [Wikipedia: Victoria 3](https://en.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Victoria_3)
- **Sphere of Influence DLC (June 24, 2024):** adds power blocs, foreign investment, interference in subjects' politics, and "The Great Game" scenario. GameStar called it the biggest and best add-on. — [GameStar](https://www.gamestar.de/artikel/victoria-3-sphere-of-influence-test-steam-dlc-review,3415362.html); [Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2411231/)
- **HoI4 historical AI:** with the "historical focus" option on, the AI follows historical focus-tree branches and events, and turning it off lets history diverge. Mods like "Historical National Focus Dates" (v1.16.10, Oct 2025) tighten the AI's timing to historical dates. Some AI war plans are hard-coded off during the historical WWII. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/1698293703771116061); [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/1730960552?p=11)

### Inferences
- **Hybrid for a single-nation sim:**
  1. HoI4-style focus trees per era serve as the strategic agenda. Mandate-era examples are "Build the Haganah," "Biltmore Program" and "Illegal Immigration (Aliyah Bet)." Each branch should have real alternatives, some of them counterfactual, such as the Uganda Scheme or a binational state.
  2. Vic3-style Journal Entries handle historical pressures. For example, "The British White Paper 1939" is active while limits are in place and resolves when certain conditions are met. Each entry can complete or fail, with timeouts.
  3. A "historical AI / historical-events" toggle governs foreign actors. Neighbors, superpowers and the British follow history unless the player's actions break the preconditions.
- **The single-nation focus lowers Vic3's "thin history" risk.** It allows HoI4-depth country-specific content without the cost of 200 nations.
- **Interests show that a mechanic must gate something important to matter.** For example, diplomatic "standing" with the US, USSR, UK and France could gate arms deals, immigration agreements and UN votes.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch focus-tree or event script syntax (Clausewitz/Jomini). Background knowledge, unverified here: HoI4 focus files use `focus = { id = ... prerequisite = ... mutually_exclusive = ... cost = N completion_reward = { ... } }`. Each focus takes 7 days per cost point (70 days for the standard cost 10). Events use `trigger`, `mean_time_to_happen` and `option` blocks.
- I did not fetch Vic3 pop and interest-group mechanics (strata, Clout, approval, law enactment chance). Background knowledge, unverified: laws are enacted over a multi-month process with success/stall/advance checks, and interest groups with Clout back or oppose laws.
- I found no 2025-2026 player-count data for Vic3 or HoI4.

## 5. Other references: Frostpunk, CK3, PeaceMaker (Israel/Palestine), Rebel Inc., Through the Darkest of Times, and others

### Takeaway
Frostpunk shows how laws can carry moral weight through two meters and permanent, branching laws. CK3 shows how stories emerge from character traits and stress. PeaceMaker (2007) is a direct Israel/Palestine precedent: dual opinion meters, random scenarios based on historical event sequences, and a critique that players learn a winning formula and repeat it. Rebel Inc. shows "stability" as balancing military and civilian priorities.

### Cited Findings
- **Frostpunk Book of Laws:** every law moves Discontent and/or Hope. There are two mutually exclusive trees, Adaptation and then Purpose, where the second forks into Order or Faith. Laws are permanent once adopted. Prolonged low Hope or high Discontent ends the game in revolt. — [Frostpunk wiki: Administration](https://frostpunk.gamepedia.com/Administration); [Wikipedia: Frostpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk)
- **Frostpunk criticism:**
  - The meter reduces morality to optimization: "how much more discontent he can take after he orders the miners to work another 24-hour shift." — [indienova critical play report](https://lab.indienova.com/indie-game-review/critical-play-report-frostpunk/)
  - Laws "only become more severe" while effects are presented neutrally, and authoritarian laws can "take care of" Hope. — [Unwinnable](https://unwinnable.com/2021/12/10/authoritarianism_in_frostpunk/)
  - Developers of Frostpunk and This War of Mine discuss moral choice design. — [Gamepressure editorial](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/fifth-dont-kill-on-moral-dilemmas-in-video-games/a-shard-of-ice-instead-of-a-heart/zf4b9)
- **CK3:**
  - Design principle: "All events and scripted content should feel relevant, impactful and immersive in relation to the underlying simulation. That way, players will perceive and remember stories - their own stories, not the developers' stories."
  - Each character has three personality traits, and stress has escalating levels with health and fertility penalties plus coping mechanisms. — [CK3 Dev Diary 31: Stress](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/ck3-dev-diary-31-a-stressful-situation.1399764/page-8); [PC Gamer review](https://pcgamer.com/crusader-kings-3-review)
  - A player criticized events as repetitive, with "choose your adventure level of depth." — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1158310/discussions/0/3808407060884649786)
- **PeaceMaker (ImpactGames; Asi Burak and Eric Brown, Carnegie Mellon):**
  - Setup: you play the Israeli PM or the Palestinian President, with two approval counters from -100 to +100, both starting at 0. As PM, the counters are Israeli and Palestinian opinion.
  - Win and loss: you lose if Palestinian opinion collapses into a new intifada, and you win when violence abates enough for two-state negotiations.
  - Polls: sub-polls cover insecurity, suppression and compassion on the Israeli side, and the President's authority, street opinion, the economy and independence on the Palestinian side.
  - Events: "black events" are violent bursts, and scenarios "pop up randomly, but many are based on sequences of events that have occurred in the past."
  - Critique: players find an effective policy set and repeat it.
  - Sources: [Wikipedia: PeaceMaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeaceMaker); [Mechanics of Magic](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2024/09/30/introducing-serious-games-peacemaker/); [Columbia](https://columbia.edu/cu/current/articles/fall2007/the-games-we-play.html)
  - An academic study (Kampf & Cuhadar) tested knowledge and attitude change with 167 Turkish, Israeli Jewish, American and Palestinian students. — [Open University of Israel PDF](https://www.openu.ac.il/innovation/chais2012/downloads/f-Kampf-Cuhadar-14_eng.pdf)
  - Sources conflict on the release year (2005 vs 2007).
- **Rebel Inc.: Escalation (Ndemic):** "balance competing military and civilian priorities to win the hearts and minds of the people," whilst stopping an insurgency. It was developed with regional politicians, journalists, charities and governments. CEO James Vaughan describes a cat-and-mouse dynamic where rebels displace to neighboring regions. — [Ndemic](https://www.ndemiccreations.com/en/54-rebel-inc-escalation); [GameBusiness.jp interview, Dec 2021](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2021/12/20/19426.html)
- **Through the Darkest of Times (Paintbucket):** a resistance group in Nazi Germany 1933-45 with weekly missions. Its storytelling was praised and its simple strategy criticized. A pre-launch daily 1933 Twitter feed built the audience. An academic interview covers procedural rhetoric. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_the_Darkest_of_Times); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=4330); [Bremen gamevironments](https://media.suub.uni-bremen.de/handle/elib/4617)

### Inferences
- **The Frostpunk lesson for Israel's charged legal and moral decisions:** examples are administrative detention, military government over Arab citizens (1948-66), the Law of Return amendments, the Haredi draft exemption, and judicial reform. These should be permanent or costly to reverse and have branching consequences. Two meters (e.g., "Social Cohesion" and "Security") must not collapse ethics into a single number to optimize; show the human cost qualitatively too.
- **PeaceMaker is the key precedent for the conflict layer.** Use multi-constituency opinion (domestic, Palestinian, international, US) and historically seeded event sequences. Its "find the formula and repeat" failure argues for diminishing returns, adaptive adversaries (as in Rebel Inc.) and era changes that invalidate old strategies.
- **The Through the Darkest of Times critique, applied here:** strong narrative cannot carry a thin strategy layer, and the reverse is true too.

### Gaps
- I gathered nothing on Tropico (edicts, faction approval, El Presidente), Papers Please (moral friction through repetitive bureaucratic UI) or This War of Mine beyond the Gamepressure title. Background knowledge, unverified here: Tropico's factions approve or disapprove of edicts and constructions and drive elections. Papers Please uses rule changes per day to show policy shifts at the street level, which is relevant to an aliyah/immigration ministry screen.
- I did not research Twilight Struggle. Background knowledge, unverified: it uses Early/Mid/Late War card decks added over time, a DEFCON track, and scoring cards. This "era decks of historical event cards" model fits the Israel timeline well.
- Other than PeaceMaker, I found no commercial Israeli or Middle East nation-management games. I did not search exhaustively; candidates such as Israeli indie titles or Hidden & Dangerous-style military games were not checked.

## 6. Time structure for a 140-year span, and blending scripted history with emergent simulation

### Takeaway
Shipped games point to an era-chaptered structure with variable time granularity: coarse during calm periods, fine during crises. Scripted history works best as condition-gated objectives that the simulation can break (Vic3 Journal Entries, HoI4 historical-focus toggle), not as fixed calendar popups. Era transitions should carry the polity forward rather than reset it (Civ VII's reversal).

### Cited Findings
- **Civ VII ran about 150-200 turns per age across three ages.** Its age-progress meter advanced each turn plus via milestones, so era length partly depends on the player. — [CivFanatics](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/)
- **Democracy uses discrete turns with per-effect inertia** ("up to 4 turns" in the example) to model lag. — [Positech modding](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/modding.html)
- **Vic3 Journal Entries gate historical content on simulation conditions**, so it can fire for any qualifying country or never fire. Players complain this loses iconic history. — [Paradox DD31](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/victoria-3-dev-diary-31-journal-entries.1508059); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/529340/discussions/0/5675129542132329045)
- **HoI4's historical focus toggle** makes the AI follow history while the player can diverge. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/1698293703771116061)
- **PeaceMaker seeded random scenarios from real historical sequences.** — [Wikipedia: PeaceMaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeaceMaker)

### Inferences
- **Recommended structure, a synthesis:**
  - About 7-9 eras as chapters, each with its own policy and building set, focus tree, journal entries and closing crisis.
  - Variable time: years-per-turn ranging from about 1 year (Ottoman period) to quarters, compressed to weeks or days during wars and crises (1948, 1967, 1973, 2023).
  - Alternatively, real-time-with-pause using Paradox-style daily ticks with monthly economic updates.
  - A 140-year span at quarterly turns is about 560 turns, too long without compression. Annual turns plus "crisis zoom" episodes come to about 140 turns plus episodes, which is manageable.
- **Ways to keep history but allow counterfactuals:**
  - **Hard anchors** are events that always happen but whose outcome depends on state. Examples: WWI and Ottoman collapse, the Holocaust, the UN vote, neighboring states' wars.
  - **Soft anchors** are Journal Entries with preconditions. Examples: the Balfour Declaration, the 1956 Suez collusion, the Camp David accords.
  - **Counterfactual branches** are focus-tree alternatives. Examples: the Uganda Scheme, a binational state, no 1967 war, a different 1977 election.
  - Present each as "History says X happened; your situation differs because Y." This makes divergence readable.
- **Mark divergence from history visibly** with a "Historical Track" meter, so players opt into realism or into alternate history.

### Gaps
- I found no source documenting a shipped game that varies turn length across eras. This is an inference.
- I found no GDC talk on blending history and simulation in this research pass. Likely candidates for follow-up: Paradox's "historical vs. sandbox" talks and Firaxis's GDC 2025 sessions on Ages.

## 7. Making complex systems readable: tooltips, advisors, dashboards

### Takeaway
The dominant patterns are Democracy's causal icon web (hover to see inputs and outputs), Paradox's nested tooltips (hover a term inside a tooltip to open another tooltip), and good tutorials. The common failure modes are notification overload and unexplained blocked actions (Vic3) and a thin, unexplained UI (Civ VII at launch).

### Cited Findings
- **Vic3 adopted CK3's nested tooltips** so players do not need "an econ degree." — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/victoria-3/nested-tooltip-system)
- **Vic3's failure modes:** a dozen or more popups piling in a corner led to ignoring them all, and blocked diplomatic actions had no clear reason. — [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/mitch-zehe/victoria-3-review/)
- **Civ VII at launch** was criticized for a "lacking UI with little explanation." Its first patch, 1.0.1, targeted UI and tooltips, and 1.3.2 (Feb 2026) improved the UI again. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VII)
- **Democracy 4's situations sit in "Department" areas of the main screen** with SVG icons color-coded green or red by the Positive flag. Policies, voter groups and situations are all icons on one screen joined by effect links. — [Positech: Situations](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/mod_situations.html); [Positech: Modding](https://www.positech.co.uk/democracy4/modding.html)
- **Civ VII Test of Time added a score per victory type** so progress across Ages is visible. — [Forbes via search summary](https://www.forbes.com/sites/barrycollins/2026/05/31/civilization-vii-finally-passes-test-of-time-as-review-scores-rise/)
- **PeaceMaker exposes the simulation through sub-polls**, broken out by security, economy and compassion. — [Wikipedia: PeaceMaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PeaceMaker)

### Inferences
- **For a ten-ministry game:**
  1. Use a Democracy-style web per ministry plus a cabinet overview.
  2. Use nested tooltips for every computed number, showing the source equation terms ("+3 from Ulpan funding, −2 from housing shortage").
  3. Add a ministers/advisors layer whose competence affects outcomes and who voice warnings in character.
  4. Use a Journal panel for active historical pressures with clear complete and fail conditions.
  5. Make notifications triaged and actionable, not stacked.
  6. Always explain why an action is blocked.

- I did not cover Suzerain's advisor UI or Tropico's almanac.
