# Sensitive History, Platforms, Ratings, Legal and Reception Risk: Commercial Steam Game Simulating the Governance of Israel (1880s to 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-09. About 25 search/fetch calls. Most hard evidence on Israel/Palestine games on Steam comes from 2024 (Fursan al-Aqsa). I found little verifiable 2025–2026 coverage specific to Israel-themed games. Several sources are partisan (Free Beacon, JNS, CAA, Al Jazeera), and this is flagged where it matters.

## 1. Precedents and their reception: what went well and what went badly

### Takeaway
The best-received titles (Through the Darkest of Times, Attentat 1942, PeaceMaker, 1979 Revolution) share four traits. Historians were involved and credited. The player is an ordinary person or a decision-maker, not a perpetrator. Atrocity is never a "win" mechanic. Perspectives are multiple or grounded in testimony. The failures came in two forms. Six Days in Fallujah drew backlash for profiting from recent trauma and for giving the other side no perspective at launch. Fursan al-Aqsa and the 2014 "Bomb Gaza" apps were seen as glorifying violence against one side and were removed. Large commercial strategy games (HOI4, Civ) mostly avoid the Holocaust and modern Israel altogether.

### Cited Findings
**Holocaust and Nazi-era games**
- *Through the Darkest of Times* (Paintbucket Games, PC January 2020): it was described as the first game published in Germany with uncensored Nazi symbols, made possible by the USK's August 2018 policy change. Earlier games had swapped the swastika for other symbols. Co-developer Jörg Friedrich criticised earlier sanitising ("Hitler was not Hitler but rather Heller. The persecuted were not Jews but some 'traitors'") — [The Local](https://www.thelocal.de/20180824/video-game-swastikas-stir-unease-in-germany); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Through_the_Darkest_of_Times)
- The German Family Minister Franziska Giffey criticised the USK decision: "You do not play with swastikas" — [The Local](https://www.thelocal.de/20180824/video-game-swastikas-stir-unease-in-germany)
- Scores: Metacritic 71, OpenCritic 73, Steam "Very Positive". PC Gamer gave 87%: "refuses to flinch from the realities of life in Nazi Germany" — [GameGeeker aggregator](https://gamegeeker.com/games/through-the-darkest-of-times); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1003090/Through_the_Darkest_of_Times/)
- The developer says it won German Developer Awards (Best Indie, Best Game Beyond Entertainment, Best Story) and was the first German Games for Impact nominee at The Game Awards 2020. This is self-reported. Concurrent players in 2024–25 were in single or low double digits, the game is often discounted 80%, and no sales figures are public — [Game Developer press release](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/the-darkest-files-released); [Steambase](https://steambase.io/apps/1003090)
- *Attentat 1942* (Charles Games, 2017): co-developed with researchers from Charles University and the Czech Academy of Sciences' Institute for Contemporary History. It includes filmed interviews with 8 survivors and won the A MAZE 2018 "Most Amazing Game" award, where the jury called it "a new form of documentary" — [Radio Prague](https://english.radio.cz/node/8170894); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=5966); [attentat1942.com/press](https://attentat1942.com/press)
- Google Play blocked Attentat 1942's mobile port for Nazi references, even after the studio appealed on the grounds of its historian collaboration and German age-rating approval. It had earlier been unavailable in Germany. Sources differ on timing relative to the 2018 USK change — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/marketing/item-2925)
- *The Light in the Darkness* (Luc Bernard, free, 2023): a linear narrative about a Polish-Jewish family in Paris up to the 1942 Vel d'Hiv roundup, deliberately without win conditions. Bernard: "there's no gamifying the Holocaust, there's no winning the Holocaust." He consulted survivors, experts and museum archives in Washington and Los Angeles, and got praise from The Times, The Economist and Radio Times (quoted on Steam, so promotional) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Light_in_the_Darkness); [CJN](https://thecjn.ca/arts/newest-take-on-holocaust-education-comes-through-a-video-game/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2520950?cc=ca)
- *Wolfenstein II* had to remove swastikas and Hitler's moustache for Germany. *Wolfenstein: Youngblood* (July 2019) was the first in the series released uncut in Germany under the social adequacy clause. Bethesda shipped both an uncut English-only version and a parallel censored German version. About 40 games with prohibited symbols had been submitted to the USK since — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/wolfenstein-youngblood/censorship); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2019/06/27/german-version-wolfenstein-youngblood-will-keep-nazi-imagery)
- *Hearts of Iron IV*: Paradox forum rules (posted about 11 years ago) state there are no death camps, no way to simulate the Holocaust or systematic purges, no swastikas, no chemical weapons, and that civilians can't be targeted directly by strategic bombing. Players criticise this as "whitewashing" and point out an inconsistency, since a Soviet "Great Purge" mechanic exists. I found no official Paradox statement of reasoning — [Paradox forum rules via devtrackers](https://devtrackers.gg/heartsofiron/p/b2bba598-hoi-iv-forum-rules-read-before-you-post); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/394360/discussions/0/1850323802579909229)
- *Civilization*: Israel has never been a playable civ, and I found no official Firaxis explanation. Fans speculate that Civ avoids existing nations, and that Jerusalem appeared as a city-state in Civ VI. Community mods (Leugi's Israel) filled the gap — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/289070/discussions/0/359543951717713852); [Civ VII discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1295660/discussions/0/598512272430668686)

**Contemporary conflict and Middle East games**
- *PeaceMaker* (ImpactGames/Carnegie Mellon, 2007): the player is the Israeli PM or the Palestinian President and aims for a negotiated settlement. CMU claims an "overwhelmingly positive response from... both sides" (promotional). A research version was used with up to 10,000 Israeli and Palestinian youth via the Peres Center. A 2006 article doubted whether it could attract players and investors — [Wikipedia: I-P conflict in video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict_in_video_games); [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/homepage/innovation/2006/fall/teaching-the-complexities-of-conflict.shtml); [J Weekly](https://jweekly.com/2006/04/07/peaceful-video-game-has-value-but-will-it-sell/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/12056286)
- *Global Conflicts: Palestine* (Serious Games Interactive, 2007): the player is a journalist, and the game was pitched as "a video game to promote peace" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict_in_video_games)
- *Under Siege* (Afkar Media, Syria, 2005): an FPS about a young Palestinian in the Second Intifada — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict_in_video_games)
- *Liyla and the Shadows of War* (Rasheed Abueideh, 2016): Apple told the developer to move it out of the Games category to "News or reference" because of its political content. After an outcry Apple reportedly reversed this about a week later — [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2016/05/18/apple-says-liyla-and-the-shadows-of-war-is-not-a-game/); [Gamasutra](https://gamasutra.com/business/apple-classifies-palestinian-developed-game-as-not-a-game-due-to-war-theme); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liyla_and_the_Shadows_of_War)
- 2014 Gaza war apps ("Bomb Gaza", "Gaza Assault: Code Red", "Whack the Hamas", "Rocket Pride") were removed from Google Play and Facebook within days for hate/threat policy violations. Amnesty UK called them "in highly questionable taste", and the ZOA called glorifying killing "on either side" deplorable — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict_in_video_games)
- *Intifada* (1989), made by a Kach supporter, was popular in Israel despite criticism from the Education Ministry — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict_in_video_games)
- *Fursan al-Aqsa* (Nidal Nijm, 2022): a Palestinian-perspective revenge shooter. Its November 2024 "Operation al-Aqsa Flood" update, with Oct 7-themed missions including the execution of unarmed soldiers, was blocked on Steam UK at the request of the UK Counter-Terrorism Internet Referral Unit. Valve said: "As with any authority for a region that oversees and governs what content can be made available, we have to comply with their requests." The game stayed on sale in the US. It was already blocked in Germany and Australia for lacking ratings — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/steam-removes-oct-7-game-at-request-of-uk-counter-terrorism-unit/); [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2024/11/28/computer-game-recreating-october-7-hamas-attack-pulled-after-police-appeal); [PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/74844/valve-pulls-controversial-shooter-from-steam-after-uk-counter-terrorism-unit-request/)
- Fursan had an "overwhelmingly positive" Steam user rating while being condemned by Rep. Ritchie Torres (December 2024 letter to Valve) and by the International Legal Forum, which argued Valve could face US anti-terror civil liability. Free Beacon claimed a Steam ban, which conflicts with other outlets' UK-only account — [Jewish Insider](https://jewishinsider.com/2024/12/steam-oct-7-roleplay-video-game-valve-violence-incitement/); [Ritchie Torres](https://ritchietorres.house.gov/posts/congressman-ritchie-torres-urges-steam-to-remove-october-7-massacre-video-game); [Free Beacon](https://freebeacon.com/latest-news/following-free-beacon-report-gaming-platform-removes-game-that-has-users-slaughter-israelis-as-a-palestinian-militant/)
- In April 2024 some Steam users called for a platform boycott over *Toofan AlAqsa*, an FPS that lets the player shoot Israeli soldiers — [The Blaze](https://www.theblaze.com/news/steam-blasted-for-selling-game-where-players-assume-role-of-palestinian-terrorists-gunning-down-israelis)
- *1979 Revolution: Black Friday* (iNK Stories, 2016): banned in Iran after release. Iranian media labelled director Navid Khonsari a spy and called the game propaganda — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/474935464); [Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2016/06/iran-black-friday-1979-revolution-game-khonsari-banned.html)
- *Six Days in Fallujah*: Konami dropped it in 2009 after an outcry from veterans and families. Highwire/Victura revived it in 2021, and it entered Early Access in June 2023. It was criticised for profiting from recent trauma ("disgusted that this is something that will be producing profit") and because players could not play as Iraqis. The developer cites consultations with more than 100 Marines and soldiers and with Iraqis (24 from Fallujah), and promised Iraqi civilian missions. Full release was reportedly pushed to late 2026 (unconfirmed) — [MCV](https://mcvuk.com/?p=140041); [Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/off-duty/movies-video-games/2023/06/22/how-soon-is-too-soon-six-days-in-fallujah-out-18-years-after-battle/); [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/six-days-fallujah-game-sparks-social-media-backlash); [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/game/4604-six-days-in-fallujah/)
- A Call of Duty precedent: Noriega sued Activision over *Black Ops II* and lost (see Legal) — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-07-16-noriega-sues-activision.html)

### Inferences
- The legitimating pattern is credited historians or survivor testimony, an ordinary-person or decision-maker perspective, and no "winning" atrocity. Attentat and PeaceMaker both used academic partners as a public shield. That shield did not fully work against algorithmic or platform reviews (Google Play's block of Attentat).
- Strategy-sim precedent (HOI4, Civ) is avoidance, not engagement. A Holocaust-inclusive grand strategy game would set new ground. The Holocaust should be represented as non-interactive narrative, testimony and consequence (Light in the Darkness / Darkest of Times model), never as a policy lever.
- What drew removals or pressure was glorification of violence against an identifiable side, especially recent atrocities (Oct 7) or "kill X" mechanics. Neutral governance, diplomacy and multi-perspective framing (the PeaceMaker model) has no record of removal.
- Commercially, serious historical games get acclaim but modest sales (Darkest of Times' low concurrent players and deep discounts). Budget for a niche audience.

### Gaps
- No primary information on *Under Ash* or *Special Force* (Hezbollah) was retrieved. No recent reception data for *Endling* or *Valiant Hearts*, and no Arma/Squad policy specifics were found.
- No official Paradox or Firaxis statements on the Holocaust or Israel omissions were found.
- No reliable sales figures for any of these titles.

## 2. Steam policies: content survey, regional restrictions, takedowns and review bombing (2023–2026), Arab/Muslim markets

### Takeaway
Steam lets developers self-disclose content and will carry controversial political games. It removes them per region when a government authority asks (the UK CTIRU for Fursan, 2024). Germany requires a valid age rating. A July 2025 rule ties allowed content to payment-processor standards, which is aimed at adult sexual content but is vaguely worded. Gulf rating bodies negotiate edits rather than ban outright, but have refused titles. I found no evidence of Israel-specific blocking.

### Cited Findings
- The content survey requires disclosure of "any mature content that players may encounter", including content present in builds but not accessible. Survey answers generate ratings from "several regional rating boards", and the Steam content rules apply "regardless of whether it is disclosed" — [Steamworks: Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)
- Germany: "all games must have an age rating in order to be offered to German customers on Steam". "Steam will no longer display games to customers in Germany if the game is missing a valid age rating." The rating can be USK or Valve's own self-rating via the survey. IARC ratings issued on third-party stores must not be entered as USK. "There are certain kinds of content that are not allowed for sale to customers in Germany" — [Steamworks: Germany](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey/germany)
- July 2025: Steamworks "What you shouldn't publish" added a bar on "content that may violate the rules and standards set forth by [Steam's] payment processors and related card networks and banks, or internet network providers." Removals of adult-only games followed (counts vary from about 22 to about 500), and there was pressure from Collective Shout — [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2025/07/valve-gets-pressured-by-payment-processors-with-a-new-rule-for-game-devs-and-various-adult-games-removed); [Checkpoint Gaming](https://checkpointgaming.net/news/2025/07/adults-only-games-are-removed-from-steam-due-to-a-crackdown-by-banks); [GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/entertainment/news/76174-valve-updates-steam-policy-with-vague-adult-content-restrictions)
- Fursan al-Aqsa UK block (November 2024) at CTIRU request. Valve said it complies with regional authorities, and the game remained available in the US — [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/steam-removes-oct-7-game-at-request-of-uk-counter-terrorism-unit/); [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Valve-removes-controversial-shooter-game-from-UK-Steam-store-after-counter-terrorism-unit-request.924267.0.html)
- Saudi GCAM age ratings since August 2016. GCAM and the UAE National Media Council usually "work hand in hand with the game publishers" to edit content rather than ban — [Saudisoft](https://localization.saudisoft.com/?p=32786); [Fast Company ME](https://fastcompanyme.com/impact/this-is-how-the-gaming-industry-is-tackling-censorship-in-the-middle-east/)
- GCAM refused classification for Final Fantasy XVI (2023) after Square Enix declined to modify it. The UAE banned Spec Ops: The Line over its depiction of Dubai and blocked distribution across the GCC, Lebanon and Jordan — [Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/news/2023/05/final-fantasy-16-refused-classification-in-saudia-arabia); [Fast Company ME](https://fastcompanyme.com/impact/this-is-how-the-gaming-industry-is-tackling-censorship-in-the-middle-east/)

### Inferences
- A game playable *as* the Israeli government, with Israeli state symbols, Zionist framing of the 1880s–1948 era and IDF operations, has a meaningful risk of refused classification or edit demands in Saudi Arabia/UAE if submitted for console or retail release there. On Steam PC, regional exposure is lower because Steam relies on self-rating, but government requests can trigger regional blocks. Plan for possible exclusion of some MENA regions and treat that revenue as upside, not baseline.
- To stay on sale in Germany, the game needs a rating via Steam's survey or the USK. Any Nazi symbols need a USK social-adequacy review (see section 3) or a censored German build.
- Platforms remove content only on a government request or a clear policy breach (hate/glorification). A balanced sim with no "kill civilians" mechanics is unlikely to be taken down. Political pressure campaigns (members of Congress, advocacy groups) do generate press even when they don't succeed.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of review bombing aimed at Israeli-made or Israel-perspective games in 2023–2026. Searches surfaced only pressure *against* pro-Palestinian games. This should not be taken as evidence that it hasn't happened.
- Steam's current review-bombing ("off-topic review activity") filter and its regional pricing/availability in Saudi Arabia/UAE/Indonesia/Malaysia/Pakistan were not verified.
- No information on payment bans tied to Israel content. The 2025 processor rule targets sexual content, and nothing indicates political content.

## 3. Ratings: ESRB, PEGI, USK (§86a / social adequacy), IARC

### Takeaway
Realistic violence against humans pushes ratings to 16/18 (PEGI) or M (ESRB). PEGI's "Discrimination" descriptor (hate-promoting stereotypes) is always PEGI 18 and "likely to infringe national criminal laws", so avoid any content that could trigger it. In Germany, Nazi symbols are allowed since August 2018 only case by case under the social adequacy clause (§86(3)/(4) StGB referenced via §86a), for critical or historical-educational works, never glorifying ones.

### Cited Findings
- PEGI Discrimination descriptor: "ethnic, religious, nationalistic or other stereotypes likely to encourage hatred". It is always PEGI 18 and "likely to infringe national criminal laws". Reportedly few or no games carry it — [Wikipedia: PEGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGI); [Games Denmark](https://gamesdenmark.dk/learn/pegi)
- PEGI violence is tiered. PEGI 7 allows only non-realistic violence. PEGI 16/18 cover realistic, detailed violence, and PEGI 18 covers "gross violence, apparent motiveless killing, or violence towards defenceless characters". PEGI generally does not contextualise, unlike some other systems — [Wikipedia: PEGI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEGI)
- USK, August 2018: previously, games with banned symbols could not even be submitted. Now they are judged case by case. "Games that critically confront the past may receive approval in the future" (USK director Elisabeth Secker), but "the general ban on symbols of unconstitutional organisations nonetheless remained in place" — [The Local](https://www.thelocal.de/20180824/video-game-swastikas-stir-unease-in-germany)
- USK deputy Lorenzo von Petersdorff: §86(4)'s social adequacy can allow NS symbolism when it is "not presented in a glorifying way" and serves a recognisable purpose such as art or historical education. The USK judges the finished product, which is why Bethesda built a parallel censored German version — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/wolfenstein-youngblood/censorship)
- Steam self-rating via the survey can satisfy Germany. IARC ratings from other stores are not USK ratings for Steam purposes — [Steamworks: Germany](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey/germany)

### Inferences
- Expected rating for a governance sim with text and illustrated depictions of the Holocaust, wars and terror attacks: likely PEGI 16 (or 12 if violence is abstract/non-visual), ESRB T or M depending on visual depiction, and USK 12–16. This is an estimate, not sourced. Abstract or text-based depiction, as in a strategy sim, keeps ratings lower than an FPS.
- For Holocaust-era content: either avoid swastikas (use documentary photos without symbols, or neutral icons) or plan a USK submission arguing social adequacy. A dedicated in-game historical/educational framing strengthens that case.
- Hamas, Hezbollah and other organisations banned in Germany (Hamas activity was banned in Germany in November 2023; flagged as background knowledge, not verified in this research) mean their symbols may face §86a issues as well. Use generic or textual representation.

### Gaps
- Official ESRB descriptor definitions and IARC questionnaire specifics were not retrieved.
- The current USK status of terrorist organisations' symbols in games was not verified.
- Israeli domestic game ratings: I found no evidence of a mandatory Israeli game rating system.

## 4. Legal: real politicians, parties, state and IDF emblems, trademarks (Israel and US)

### Takeaway
In the US, depicting real political or historical figures in an expressive game is strongly protected (Noriega v. Activision dismissed on First Amendment and transformative-use grounds). In Israel, the right of publicity is judge-made. It is grounded in the Privacy Protection Law §2(6) (commercial use of name, picture or voice without consent) and unjust enrichment, and survives death for at least 25 years (Dali estate case). Use of Israel's state flag and emblem commercially requires a permit under the 1949 Flag and Emblem Law. Get Israeli counsel before using real living figures' likenesses in marketing, or official emblems on box art.

### Cited Findings
- *Noriega v. Activision* (LA Superior Court, Judge Fahey, 2014): anti-SLAPP motion granted and the case dismissed with prejudice. The First Amendment outweighed the right of publicity under the transformative use test, given that Noriega was a minor part of a larger expressive work (two missions, under 30 lines). The court rejected the Ninth Circuit's narrow likeness-only analysis (Keller/NCAA). It was a trial-level ruling — [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/litigation-alert-california-superior-court-finds-use-of-likeness-of-former-panamanian-dictator-manuel-noriega-in-video-game-transformative); [Duke L&T Review](https://dltr.law.duke.edu/2015/11/17/noriega-v-activisionblizzard-the-first-amendment-right-to-use-a-historical-figures-likeness-in-video-games/); [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/media-law-monitor/2014/11/transformative-use-of-notorious-dictators-name-and)
- Israel: the Privacy Protection Law §2(6) covers use of a person's "name, nickname, picture or voice for commercial purposes" without consent. In Aloniel v. McDonald's (CA 8483/02, 2004), the Supreme Court recognised the right of publicity as an economic right enforceable via unjust enrichment — [FBC Lawyers](https://www.fbclawyers.com/news/legal-update-right-publicity-israeli-law-september-2016)
- Dali estate (Central District Court, Justice Makover, August 2016): the publicity right survives death for at least 25 years and is a transferable, judicially created property right — [FBC Lawyers](https://www.fbclawyers.com/news/legal-update-right-publicity-israeli-law-september-2016); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/trademark/589214/salvador-dalis-publicity-right-found-to-be-alive-and-kicking)
- Bar Rafaeli v. Samsung importer (Tel Aviv District Court, 2013): about $113,400 damages plus costs for unauthorised advertising use — [Jewish Press](https://www.jewishpress.com/news/israels-supermodel-bar-rafaeli-wins-113000-in-samsung-lawsuit/2013/10/17/)
- The Flag and Emblem Law (1949) requires a license or permit from the Interior Minister for commercial use of the state flag and for manufacturing objects bearing the state emblem. The Protection of Symbols Law (1974) also protects declared official symbols (secondary description, statute not read). The Trade Marks Ordinance (1972) bars marks suggesting presidential connection — [OICP Protocolo](https://www.oicp-protocolo.com/israel/); [Mondaq trademarks guide](https://www.mondaq.com/intellectual-property/1055782/trademarks-comparative-guide)

### Inferences
- Depicting real prime ministers and leaders (Herzl, Ben-Gurion, Begin, Rabin, Netanyahu, Arafat and others) as characters within an expressive historical sim is likely defensible in the US. In Israel the Aloniel and Dali precedents create more risk, especially for using the faces of living figures (or those dead under 25 years) in *marketing* such as box art, trailers and store capsules. Mitigations: portraits drawn by original artists rather than photos, factual or attributable statements, no fabricated defamatory quotes about living people, and a disclaimer. Avoid putting real living politicians in key art.
- Real party names (Likud, Labor, Mapai, Fatah, Hamas) used descriptively are likely nominative use. Party *logos* may be trademarks or copyrighted, so use stylised generic icons.
- The state emblem and flag on the store page or box art may need an Interior Ministry permit under the 1949 law. Using the flag *within* the game as historical depiction is a grey area that needs counsel. IDF emblem protection is unverified, so use non-official stylisation.
- Defamation of living persons (for example, attributing crimes or corruption to named living politicians in events) is the highest legal-risk area. Israel's Defamation Law and its defences (truth plus public interest) were not researched here.

### Gaps
- No Israeli case found specifically on video games and real politicians. The Israeli Defamation (Prohibition) Law 1965 and the IDF insignia rules were not researched.
- Whether Noriega was appealed was not confirmed.

## 5. Best practices: advisory boards, sensitivity readers, content warnings, educational partnerships, multiple perspectives, avoiding gamified atrocity

### Takeaway
Well-received precedents credit named academic partners (Charles University / Czech Academy of Sciences for Attentat; CMU / Peres Center for PeaceMaker), use survivor or witness testimony, and consult the "other side" (Six Days in Fallujah's Iraqi consultants were added after backlash). They also refuse "win" states for atrocities (The Light in the Darkness). Commercial grand-strategy convention (HOI4) bans mechanics that let players commit genocide, and civilians cannot be directly targeted.

### Cited Findings
- Attentat 1942: "researched and written by a team of professional historians", with 8 survivor interviews and academic partners credited — [attentat1942.com](https://attentat1942.com/press); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=5966)
- The Light in the Darkness: survivor and expert consultation, Holocaust museum archives, and no win condition ("no winning the Holocaust") — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Light_in_the_Darkness); [CJN](https://thecjn.ca/arts/newest-take-on-holocaust-education-comes-through-a-video-game/)
- PeaceMaker: dual playable leadership (Israeli PM or Palestinian President), and a study program with Israeli and Palestinian youth through the Peres Center — [CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/homepage/innovation/2006/fall/teaching-the-complexities-of-conflict.shtml); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_conflict_in_video_games)
- Six Days in Fallujah: absence of the Iraqi perspective was a core criticism, and consultation with Fallujah residents and promised Iraqi civilian missions were later responses — [Worthplaying](https://worthplaying.com/game/4604-six-days-in-fallujah/); [Middle East Eye](https://www.middleeasteye.net/news/six-days-fallujah-game-sparks-social-media-backlash)
- HOI4 design rules: no Holocaust simulation, no death camps, no direct targeting of civilians — [Paradox forum rules](https://devtrackers.gg/heartsofiron/p/b2bba598-hoi-iv-forum-rules-read-before-you-post)
- Germany's industry body valued the uncut Wolfenstein ruling as allowing games to be "part of the social discourse in the face of... anti-Semitism" — [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2019/06/27/german-version-wolfenstein-youngblood-will-keep-nazi-imagery)

### Inferences (recommended practices for this project)
- **Advisory board**: credit named historians covering Zionist/Yishuv history, the Holocaust, Palestinian history (including the Nakba), and Mizrahi/Arab-Israeli history. Include at least one Palestinian or Arab historian and one Israeli. Publish the board on the Steam page.
- **Sensitivity readers** from multiple communities: Israeli Jewish (Ashkenazi and Mizrahi), Palestinian (West Bank, Gaza, diaspora), Arab citizens of Israel, Druze, Haredi, and Holocaust survivor descendants.
- **The Holocaust as non-interactive testimony and consequence**: the player governs the Yishuv, and the Holocaust appears as historical events, refugee and immigration crises (illegal immigration under the White Paper), and trauma that shapes politics. Never a mechanic the player can affect by optimising.
- **Terror attacks and wars** shown as events with human cost (named victims are risky, so use aggregated or composite figures) and policy dilemmas. No player-executed atrocities. Collective punishment, settlement and occupation choices should carry modelled costs (international, moral, demographic) without being turned into a points game.
- **Dual or plural perspectives** following the PeaceMaker model. Consider playable or narrated Palestinian leadership viewpoints, or "voices" panels presenting contested narratives (for example 1948 as Independence and as Nakba) with sources.
- **Content warnings** at launch and per chapter, an option to hide graphic imagery, and links to educational resources.
- **Educational partnerships** (Yad Vashem, USHMM, Palestinian oral-history archives): no evidence was gathered that these institutions partner with commercial games. Treat as aspirational and approach early.

### Gaps
- No sources were retrieved on formal sensitivity-reading practices in games, on Yad Vashem's or USHMM's policy on game partnerships, or on academic papers (for example Chapman and Linderoth on Holocaust games, or Kingsepp). These should be added if possible.

## 6. Reception risk: review bombing, post-Oct 7 press, creators, funding

### Takeaway
Post-Oct 7, the documented flashpoints were games seen as glorifying violence against Israelis (Fursan al-Aqsa, Toofan AlAqsa). These drew advocacy pressure, a member of Congress, and government-requested regional removals. At the same time Fursan held an "overwhelmingly positive" user rating, which shows polarised audiences can rally behind a game. An Israel governance sim will be attacked from both directions. Pro-Palestinian audiences may read it as normalising occupation or "playing Israel". Pro-Israel audiences may object to any Nakba, occupation or civilian-harm depiction. Evidence-backed framing and multi-perspective design are the main mitigations.

### Inferences
- **The 2023–2026 period, especially Oct 7 and the Gaza war**, is the riskiest segment. Consider ending playable content at an earlier cutoff, or treating post-2023 years with heavy restraint (no Oct 7 tactical gameplay, factual event cards, sourced casualty data from multiple bodies, clear attribution). Six Days in Fallujah's "too soon" lesson applies even more strongly to an ongoing conflict.
- **Review bombing** risk is high in both directions. Steam's off-topic review filter is a partial mitigation (not verified in this research). Pre-launch community management, a transparent "historical sources" page, and an advisory board reduce the "propaganda" attack surface.
- **Streamers**: creators may avoid the game due to audience polarisation, or pick it up as "controversial content". No data was found.
- **Funding**: publisher appetite is likely low given Konami's precedent and post-2023 polarisation. Self-publishing or a niche serious-games or strategy publisher is more realistic. Cultural or educational grants are possible if educational framing is credible. No direct evidence was found on 2024–2026 publisher attitudes.
- **Developer safety**: if team members are Israeli or Palestinian, plan for harassment risk (the 1979 Revolution precedent).

### Gaps
- No verified 2025–2026 examples of Israel-perspective games or Israeli studios facing review bombing or boycotts. Searches returned only pro-Palestinian-game controversies.
- No coverage found from Haaretz or Times of Israel on Israeli game developers post-Oct 7. No data on streamer behaviour or publisher funding appetite.
