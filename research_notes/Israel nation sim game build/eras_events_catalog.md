## Key Question 1: Era boundaries and the governing structure the player controls in each era

### Takeaway
The period splits into **17 playable eras**. They fall into two structural phases.

- **Pre-state, 1882–1948.** The player runs a voluntary, non-sovereign proto-government: the Zionist Organization (ZO) and Palestine Office, then the Jewish Agency and the Va'ad Leumi with the Histadrut and the Haganah. It works under an imperial sovereign, Ottoman and then British. Its levers are fundraising, land purchase, immigration certificates, diplomacy with the empire, and a militia it controls only partly.
- **Sovereign Israel, May 1948 onward.** The player is a prime minister who needs a Knesset coalition. Levers are the cabinet ministries, the IDF, the budget, and the Bank of Israel (independent after 1985). The player is constrained by the courts, the Attorney General, coalition partners, and US patronage (from 1967–73).

### Cited Findings

#### ERA 1 — Ottoman Palestine: First and Second Aliyah (1882–1914)
- **GOV:** No unified Jewish authority. The relevant actors were philanthropists (Baron Edmond de Rothschild's administration of the colonies), Hovevei Zion, and from 1897 the **World Zionist Organization** (Herzl, Basel Congress). The ZO opened its **Palestine Office** in Jaffa in 1908 under Arthur Ruppin. A game should model the player as "ZO Executive / Palestine Office". Levers: land purchase through the Jewish National Fund (1901), settlement funding, and Ottoman lobbying. — [Britannica: Zionism](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Zionism); [Wikipedia: Zionist Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Zionist_Organization)
- **First Aliyah (1882–1903):** About 25,000–35,000 immigrants, mostly from the Russian Empire and Romania after the 1881–82 pogroms (Bilu, Hovevei Zion). They founded Rishon LeZion, Zikhron Ya'akov and Rosh Pinna (1882). Several colonies failed economically and survived only on Rothschild subsidies. — [Wikipedia: First Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Aliyah)
- **Second Aliyah (1904–1914):** About 35,000–40,000 arrivals, mostly young socialist Zionists; many later emigrated. Milestones: Degania, the first kvutza (1909–10); Tel Aviv (founded as Ahuzat Bayit, 1909); the Hashomer guard organization (1909); the struggle for "Hebrew labor"; the revival of Hebrew; the "War of the Languages" (Technion, 1913). — [Wikipedia: Second Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Aliyah)
- **DP — the Uganda Scheme (1903–05):** Britain offered territory in East Africa. Herzl backed it as a "night shelter" after the Kishinev pogrom. Russian Zionists threatened a split, and the 7th Zionist Congress rejected it in 1905. The Territorialists, led by Zangwill, left. **Counterfactual branch:** accept the offer and found an East African autonomy. — [Wikipedia: Uganda Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Scheme)
- **DOM (Ottoman era):** security (Bedouin and village raids, guard labor disputes); land purchases from absentee effendi landlords displaced Arab tenant farmers, an early source of conflict [CONTESTED in scale]; Ottoman restrictions on Jewish immigration and land sales; malaria and health; Hebrew education; Old Yishuv versus New Yishuv religious tensions. — [Wikipedia: First Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Aliyah); [Britannica: Israel – history](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel/History)

#### ERA 2 — World War I and the Balfour Declaration (1914–1920)
- **GOV:** The ZO, split across the warring powers (Berlin office versus Weizmann in London). Ottoman wartime repression under Djemal Pasha included expulsions from Jaffa and Tel Aviv in 1917, deportation of non-Ottoman Jews, and famine and locusts in 1915. — [Britannica: Israel – history](https://www.britannica.com/place/Israel/History)
- **EVT:** the NILI spy ring for Britain, exposed in 1917, a dilemma for the Yishuv leadership, which feared Ottoman reprisals; the **Jewish Legion** (1917–18); the **Balfour Declaration** (2 November 1917), a "national home for the Jewish people" with a proviso protecting "the civil and religious rights of existing non-Jewish communities"; the **Sykes–Picot Agreement** (1916); and the **Hussein–McMahon correspondence** (1915–16). Arab nationalists read the McMahon letters as promising Palestine to the Arabs; Britain later denied this. [CONTESTED] — [Wikipedia: Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration); [Wikipedia: Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement); [Wikipedia: McMahon–Hussein Correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McMahon%E2%80%93Hussein_Correspondence)
- **DP:** Whether to bet the Yishuv's survival on Britain (Weizmann; NILI; the Legion) or stay loyal to the Ottomans. Ben-Gurion and Ben-Zvi initially chose "Ottomanization" and were deported anyway. — [Wikipedia: NILI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nili)
- **EVT, Arab perspective:** The Palestinian Arab congresses (from 1919) and the King–Crane Commission (1919) recorded broad Arab opposition to Zionism and to separating Palestine from Syria. — [Wikipedia: King–Crane Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King%E2%80%93Crane_Commission)

#### ERA 3 — Early British Mandate: building the "state in the making" (1920–1929)
- **GOV:** Civil administration began under High Commissioner Herbert Samuel (1920). The League of Nations confirmed the Mandate in July 1922, with Article 4 recognizing a "Jewish agency". The Yishuv's self-government had three pillars: the **Assembly of Representatives (Asefat HaNivharim, 1920)**, which elected the **Va'ad Leumi** (National Council); the **Zionist Executive**, which became the **Jewish Agency** (expanded 1929); and the **Histadrut** (1920), a labor federation that also owned enterprises and ran health care (Kupat Holim), bank, construction (Solel Boneh) and other services. The **Haganah** (1920) was nominally under civilian control. The Rabbinate was created in 1921. **Game note:** this is a "multi-headed" government; the player should negotiate with the Histadrut and the parties instead of commanding them. — [Wikipedia: Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine); [Wikipedia: Jewish National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_National_Council); [Wikipedia: Histadrut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histadrut); [Wikipedia: Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah)
- **EVT:** the **Nebi Musa riots** in Jerusalem (April 1920: 5 Jews and 4 Arabs killed); the **Jaffa riots** (May 1921: 47 Jews and 48 Arabs killed); the **Churchill White Paper** (1922), which separated Transjordan from the national home area and introduced "economic absorptive capacity" as the test for immigration. Others: Third Aliyah (1919–23, about 40,000), Fourth Aliyah (1924–29, about 80,000, middle class from Poland), Hebrew University (1925), the 1926–27 economic crisis and emigration. — [Wikipedia: 1920 Nebi Musa riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_Nebi_Musa_riots); [Wikipedia: Jaffa riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_riots); [Wikipedia: Churchill White Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churchill_White_Paper); [Wikipedia: Fourth Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Aliyah)
- **1929 riots:** The trigger was a dispute over the Western Wall. In August, 133 Jews were killed, including 67 in the **Hebron massacre**, and 116 Arabs were killed, mostly by British forces. The ancient Jewish community of Hebron was evacuated. The **Passfield White Paper (1930)** proposed restricting immigration; Zionist pressure led to the MacDonald letter (1931), which reversed it in effect. — [Wikipedia: 1929 Palestine riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_Palestine_riots); [Wikipedia: Passfield White Paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passfield_white_paper)
- **DOM:** The Revisionists (Jabotinsky, 1925) and Labor clashed over strategy and the "Iron Wall". There were religious–secular arrangements, Jewish labor versus Arab labor conflict, land purchases (the Jezreel Valley/Sursuk purchases displaced tenants [CONTESTED]), and crises in British trust.

#### ERA 4 — Fifth Aliyah, Arab Revolt and the White Paper (1929–1939)
- **GOV:** As in Era 3. Ben-Gurion chaired the Jewish Agency Executive from 1935. The Haganah grew, and the **Irgun** split from it (1931; 1937). The rivalry between Labor and the Revisionists became violent: Chaim Arlosoroff was assassinated in 1933, and the case is still unsolved [CONTESTED]. — [Wikipedia: Chaim Arlosoroff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaim_Arlosoroff); [Wikipedia: Irgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun)
- **Fifth Aliyah (about 1929–39):** roughly 250,000 immigrants, many from Germany after 1933. — [Wikipedia: Fifth Aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Aliyah)
- **DP — the Haavara (Transfer) Agreement (1933):** The Zionist movement and Nazi Germany agreed to let German Jews transfer part of their assets as German goods. The alternative was to join the Jewish boycott of Germany. The agreement brought in about 60,000 people and capital, but was attacked as breaking the boycott. — [Wikipedia: Haavara Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haavara_Agreement)
- **Arab Revolt (1936–39):** a general strike followed by an armed revolt against British rule and Jewish immigration. Britain suppressed it harshly, and estimates of Arab deaths run to about 5,000. Roughly 300–400 Jews and about 260 British were killed [figures vary]. Responses: the Haganah's "havlagah" (restraint) policy, the Special Night Squads (Orde Wingate), the "Tower and Stockade" settlements, and Irgun bombings in Arab markets. — [Wikipedia: 1936–1939 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine)
- **DP — the Peel Commission (July 1937):** It proposed the first partition, a small Jewish state (about 20% of the land) plus population transfer. Ben-Gurion and Weizmann accepted it in principle as a basis for negotiation, over strong internal opposition. The Arab Higher Committee rejected it, and Britain dropped it (Woodhead Commission, 1938). **Branch:** accept Peel and fight for its implementation, or reject it. — [Wikipedia: Peel Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peel_Commission)
- **White Paper (May 1939):** It capped Jewish immigration at 75,000 over five years, with Arab consent required after that. It restricted land purchases and promised an independent Palestine with an Arab majority within 10 years. It came on the eve of the Holocaust. — [Wikipedia: White Paper of 1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Paper_of_1939)
- **Palestinian perspective:** The revolt is remembered as a national uprising against colonial rule and dispossession. Its suppression (leaders exiled, disarmament) left Palestinian society weakened in 1947–48. — [Interactive Encyclopedia of the Palestine Question (IPS/Palestinian Museum)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_Encyclopedia_of_the_Palestine_Question)

#### ERA 5 — World War II, the Holocaust and the revolt against Britain (1939–1947)
- **GOV:** Jewish Agency and Va'ad Leumi. Armed forces now included the **Haganah/Palmach** (Palmach founded 1941), the **Irgun** (Begin from 1943), and **Lehi** (Stern, 1940). The player has limited control over the dissident groups.
- **DP — the "two wars" doctrine:** Ben-Gurion's line was "We will fight the war as if there were no White Paper, and fight the White Paper as if there were no war." About 30,000 Yishuv volunteers joined the British forces, and the **Jewish Brigade** was formed in 1944. Lehi chose to fight Britain during the war and even approached Axis powers. — [Wikipedia: Jewish Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Brigade); [Wikipedia: Lehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehi_(militant_group))
- **Biltmore Program (May 1942, New York):** It demanded a Jewish "commonwealth" and open immigration under Jewish Agency control. This marked a shift toward the US and toward statehood. — [Wikipedia: Biltmore Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biltmore_Conference)
- **Holocaust:** About six million Jews were murdered. The Yishuv's rescue capacity was limited. The record of the leadership's response (rescue missions, Hannah Szenes and the paratroopers, the "blood for goods" proposal through Joel Brand) has been heavily debated since [CONTESTED: Tom Segev, *The Seventh Million*, versus defenders]. — [Wikipedia: Joel Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Brand); [Wikipedia: Hannah Szenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannah_Szenes)
- **Refugee-ship disasters:** the *Patria* (November 1940), sunk by a Haganah bomb meant to disable it, about 267 killed; the *Struma* (February 1942), sunk in the Black Sea, about 768 killed. — [Wikipedia: Struma disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Struma_disaster); [Wikipedia: Patria disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patria_disaster)
- **DP — the "Hunting Season" (Saison) (November 1944–1945):** After Lehi assassinated Lord Moyne in Cairo, the Agency and Haganah handed Irgun members to the British. Begin chose not to retaliate, which avoided a civil war. **Branch:** retaliate, or have the Agency tolerate the dissidents. — [Wikipedia: The Saison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Saison)
- **Jewish Resistance Movement (October 1945–August 1946):** a Haganah–Irgun–Lehi coalition against Britain after the Labour government kept the White Paper. Events: the Night of the Bridges (June 1946), Operation Agatha/"Black Sabbath" (29 June 1946, mass arrests of Agency leaders), and the **King David Hotel bombing** (22 July 1946, 91 killed), after which the Haganah left the coalition. Others: the hanging of Irgun members and the Irgun's sergeants affair (1947). — [Wikipedia: Jewish Resistance Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_resistance_movement); [Wikipedia: King David Hotel bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_David_Hotel_bombing)
- **Aliyah Bet (clandestine immigration):** About 100,000+ people arrived on more than 100 ships. Britain interned more than 50,000 in Cyprus. The *Exodus 1947* affair (July 1947) became a global public-relations crisis for Britain. — [Wikipedia: Aliyah Bet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliyah_Bet); [Wikipedia: SS Exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Exodus)
- **Partition:** UNSCOP (1947) led to **UNGA Resolution 181** (29 November 1947; 33–13–10). The plan gave about 56% of the land to the Jewish state and placed Jerusalem under an international regime. The Jewish Agency accepted; the Arab Higher Committee and the Arab states rejected. — [Wikipedia: UN Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine)

#### ERA 6 — The 1947–49 War: independence and the Nakba
- **GOV:** In the transition, the People's Council and People's Administration became the **Provisional State Council and Provisional Government** (Ben-Gurion as PM and defense minister). The **IDF** was established on 26 May 1948. — [Wikipedia: Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence); [Wikipedia: Provisional government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_government_of_Israel)
- **Phases:** Civil war phase (November 1947–May 1948): the siege of Jerusalem, convoys, and **Plan Dalet** (March 1948). **Deir Yassin** (9 April 1948, about 107–110 Arab villagers killed by Irgun and Lehi; figures debated) and the **Hadassah convoy massacre** (13 April, 78 killed). The state was declared on 14 May 1948. Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Iraq and Lebanon then invaded. Truces followed, then Operations Danny, Hiram and Horev, and the **1949 armistice lines** (the "Green Line"). About 6,000 Israelis were killed, roughly 1% of the Jewish population. — [Wikipedia: 1948 Palestine war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_war); [Wikipedia: Deir Yassin massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Yassin_massacre); [Wikipedia: 1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements)
- **Nakba:** About 700,000 Palestinian Arabs (estimates range from about 520,000 to more than 750,000) fled or were expelled. More than 400 villages were depopulated and destroyed. Israel barred return, and the Absentee Property Law followed in 1950. **[CONTESTED — causes]:** Benny Morris's *Birth of the Palestinian Refugee Problem* attributes it to a mix of war-induced flight, expulsions in some places (for example Lydda and Ramle, July 1948), and the Yishuv's decision against return, without a single master plan. Palestinian historians (Walid Khalidi; Ilan Pappé, *Ethnic Cleansing of Palestine*) argue systematic expulsion through Plan Dalet. Older Israeli narratives stressed Arab leaders' calls to flee, a claim later historians largely discounted. — [Wikipedia: Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba); [Wikipedia: 1948 Palestinian expulsion and flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight); [IPS / PalQuest (village entries based on Khalidi's *All That Remains*)](https://www.palestine-studies.org/en/print/1652751)
- **DP — declare the state on 14 May 1948 or accept a US trusteeship and delay:** The US State Department (Marshall) urged delay. The People's Administration voted 6–4 to declare. — [Wikipedia: Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence)
- **DP — the *Altalena* (June 1948):** An Irgun arms ship arrived during the truce. Ben-Gurion ordered it seized or shelled: 16 Irgun fighters and 3 IDF soldiers were killed. Begin ordered his men not to fire back, and a single army was established. **Branch:** compromise on arms allocation, or civil war. — [Wikipedia: Altalena Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altalena_Affair)
- **Others:** the dissolution of the Palmach staff (November 1948); the assassination of UN mediator **Folke Bernadotte** by Lehi (September 1948); the decision not to take the West Bank and the Old City (Ben-Gurion's "bechiya ledorot", "a weeping for generations") [CONTESTED as a missed opportunity versus a demographic choice]. — [Wikipedia: Folke Bernadotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folke_Bernadotte)

#### ERA 7 — Mass immigration and austerity (1949–1956)
- **GOV:** A sovereign parliamentary state. Ben-Gurion led Mapai-dominated coalitions with the religious parties and the centrists. The first Knesset was elected in January 1949. The **Harari Decision** (1950) chose to write the constitution through Basic Laws over time and dropped a single constitution, because of religious–secular disagreement. — [Knesset: Harari Decision / Basic Laws](https://main.knesset.gov.il/en/activity/pages/basiclaws.aspx); [Wikipedia: Harari Decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harari_Decision)
- **Immigration:** The population roughly doubled by 1951, with about 690,000 arrivals from 1948 to 1951. These included Holocaust survivors, Operation Magic Carpet from Yemen (about 49,000), Operation Ezra and Nehemiah from Iraq (about 120,000), and later Morocco. **Ma'abarot** transit camps housed about 250,000 people at their peak. **Law of Return** (1950). — [Wikipedia: Ma'abarot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma%27abarot); [Wikipedia: Operation Ezra and Nehemiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Ezra_and_Nehemiah); [Wikipedia: Law of Return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Return)
- **Economy — Tzena (austerity, 1949–59):** Food and goods were rationed, and a black market grew. The 1952 "New Economic Policy" devalued the currency. — [Wikipedia: Austerity in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austerity_in_Israel)
- **DP — Reparations Agreement with West Germany (1952):** Germany paid DM 3 billion to Israel plus DM 450 million to the Claims Conference. Ben-Gurion pushed it through. Begin led a violent protest outside the Knesset (January 1952), and the Knesset approved it 61–50. **Branch:** refuse "blood money" and give up the capital that later underwrote infrastructure. — [Wikipedia: Reparations Agreement between Israel and West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reparations_Agreement_between_Israel_and_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany)
- **Arab citizens:** **Military government** over Arab-populated areas from 1948 to 1966 (travel permits, curfews); land expropriations. — [Wikipedia: Arab citizens of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_citizens_of_Israel)
- **Security:** Fedayeen infiltration and reprisal raids. **Qibya** (October 1953, Unit 101 under Sharon, 69 villagers killed, most of them women and children) drew international condemnation. — [Wikipedia: Qibya massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qibya_massacre)
- **Scandals and trials:** **The Lavon Affair** (1954): Operation Susannah, a failed Israeli false-flag bombing campaign in Egypt. Who gave the order became a political crisis lasting into the 1960s and contributed to Ben-Gurion's 1963 resignation and the Rafi split. The **Kasztner trial** (1954–55): Judge Halevi ruled that Kasztner had "sold his soul to the devil". Kasztner was assassinated in 1957, and the Supreme Court largely reversed the ruling in 1958. The government fell over the case in 1955. — [Wikipedia: Lavon Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavon_Affair); [Wikipedia: Rudolf Kastner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Kastner)
- **Contested social issue:** the **Yemenite Children Affair** (allegations that immigrant infants disappeared, 1948–54); three inquiries, ongoing dispute. [CONTESTED] — [Wikipedia: Yemenite Children Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemenite_Children_affair)
- **Religion–state:** the Status Quo Letter (June 1947: Shabbat, kashrut, personal status, separate education streams); yeshiva student deferments (initially about 400). — [Wikipedia: Status quo (Israel)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_quo_(Israel))

#### ERA 8 — Suez, the French alliance and state-building (1956–1967)
- **Suez/Sinai Campaign (29 October 1956):** a secret coordination with Britain and France (the **Protocol of Sèvres**). Israel conquered Sinai and withdrew in March 1957 under US and Soviet pressure, in exchange for UNEF and freedom of navigation through Tiran. — [Wikipedia: Suez Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suez_Crisis)
- **Kafr Qasim massacre (29 October 1956):** Border Police killed 48–49 Arab citizens returning from work after a curfew they had not been told about. The trial produced the doctrine of the "manifestly illegal order". — [Wikipedia: Kafr Qasim massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Qasim_massacre)
- **Dimona:** French assistance (agreed around 1957) for a reactor in the Negev. JFK pressed for inspections, and the "nuclear ambiguity" posture began. — [Wikipedia: Negev Nuclear Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Nuclear_Research_Center)
- **Others:** the **Wadi Salib riots** (1959; Mizrahi discrimination protest in Haifa); the capture of **Eichmann** (1960), his trial (1961) and execution (1962); the National Water Carrier (1964), which Arab states countered with a diversion plan, leading to the "War over Water"; diplomatic relations with West Germany (1965); the end of military government (1966); the 1966–67 recession ("Mitun"); Eshkol succeeded Ben-Gurion (1963); Africa outreach through MASHAV (1958). — [Wikipedia: Wadi Salib riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_Salib_riots); [Wikipedia: Eichmann trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eichmann_trial); [Wikipedia: National Water Carrier of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Water_Carrier_of_Israel)

#### ERA 9 — The Six-Day War and its aftermath (1967–1970)
- **The waiting period (May–June 1967):** Egypt expelled UNEF, closed the Straits of Tiran (22 May) and signed a pact with Jordan. There was public pressure on PM Eshkol. A national unity government was formed with Dayan as defense minister and Begin as minister without portfolio. — [Wikipedia: Six-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War)
- **War (5–10 June 1967):** Israel took Sinai, Gaza, the West Bank, East Jerusalem and the Golan. About 280,000–325,000 Palestinians were displaced (the **Naksa**). Jerusalem's municipal boundaries were expanded and East Jerusalem annexed in effect. — [Wikipedia: Six-Day War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Six-Day_War); [Wikipedia: Naksa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naksa)
- **Post-war:** the **Khartoum Resolution** (September 1967, "three no's"); **UNSC 242** (November 1967, "land for peace", with an ambiguity over "territories" versus "the territories") [CONTESTED interpretation]; the **Allon Plan** (unofficial, territorial compromise with Jordan); the first settlements (Kfar Etzion; Hebron/Kiryat Arba, 1968). — [Wikipedia: Khartoum Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khartoum_Resolution); [Wikipedia: UNSC Resolution 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242); [Wikipedia: Allon Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allon_Plan)
- **War of Attrition (1967/69–August 1970):** Egyptian artillery against the Bar-Lev Line; Israeli deep-penetration bombing; Soviet pilots engaged (Operation Rimon 20, July 1970). It ended with a US-brokered ceasefire (Rogers initiative). Gahal quit the unity government over accepting it. — [Wikipedia: War of Attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Attrition)
- **Palestinian perspective:** occupation, military rule of about 1 million Palestinians; the rise of the PLO under Arafat (Karameh 1968); the beginning of land confiscation for settlements. — [Wikipedia: Israeli-occupied territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli-occupied_territories)

#### ERA 10 — Golda Meir, Munich and the Yom Kippur War (1970–1977)
- **EVT:** the Munich Olympics massacre (September 1972, 11 Israelis killed; Operation Wrath of God); the Israeli Black Panthers (Mizrahi protest, 1971); Lod Airport massacre (1972); Libyan Arab Airlines Flight 114 shot down (February 1973, 108 killed). — [Wikipedia: Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre); [Wikipedia: Israeli Black Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Black_Panthers)
- **Yom Kippur War (6–25 October 1973):** Egypt and Syria launched a surprise attack. Mobilization was delayed by "the Concept" (an intelligence assumption that Egypt would not attack without air superiority). About 2,650–2,700 Israelis were killed. The US airlifted supplies (Operation Nickel Grass). The war ended with the IDF across the canal and near Damascus; the Arab oil embargo followed. — [Wikipedia: Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War)
- **Agranat Commission (interim report April 1974):** It blamed the military (Chief of Staff Elazar and intelligence chief Zeira resigned) and cleared the politicians. Public protest (Motti Ashkenazi) led to Meir's resignation (April 1974). — [Wikipedia: Agranat Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agranat_Commission)
- **Diplomacy:** Kissinger's shuttle diplomacy; Sinai I (1974), Syria disengagement (1974), Sinai II (1975). **UNGA Resolution 3379** ("Zionism is racism", 1975; revoked in 1991). — [Wikipedia: UNGA Resolution 3379](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_3379)
- **Others:** the **Entebbe raid** (July 1976); **Land Day** (30 March 1976, 6 Arab citizens killed protesting land expropriation in the Galilee); Gush Emunim settlement drive (Sebastia, 1974–75); Rabin resigned (1977) over his wife's US dollar account. — [Wikipedia: Operation Entebbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Entebbe); [Wikipedia: Land Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Day); [Wikipedia: Gush Emunim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gush_Emunim)

#### ERA 11 — Begin: the "Mahapakh", peace with Egypt and Lebanon (1977–1984)
- **GOV:** The first right-wing government (Likud with the religious parties and DMC) after the May 1977 election ("the upheaval"). — [Wikipedia: 1977 Israeli legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Israeli_legislative_election)
- **Peace with Egypt:** Sadat came to Jerusalem (November 1977). The **Camp David Accords** (September 1978) were followed by the **peace treaty** (26 March 1979). All of Sinai was returned, and **Yamit** was evacuated (April 1982). A Palestinian autonomy framework was never implemented. Egypt was suspended from the Arab League. — [Wikipedia: Camp David Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David_Accords); [Wikipedia: Egypt–Israel peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Israel_peace_treaty)
- **Others:** the Coastal Road massacre (March 1978, 38 killed), which led to Operation Litani; **Operation Opera** (Osirak reactor, June 1981); the **Golan Heights Law** (December 1981); Operation Moses (Ethiopian Jews, 1984–85); the Jewish Underground (exposed 1984). — [Wikipedia: Operation Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Opera); [Wikipedia: Jewish Underground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Underground)
- **1982 Lebanon War (from 6 June 1982):** The stated goal was a 40 km zone. Sharon expanded it to Beirut, to evict the PLO and install Bashir Gemayel. After Gemayel's assassination, Phalangist militia committed the **Sabra and Shatila massacre** (September 1982; estimates range from several hundred to about 3,500 [CONTESTED]). The **Kahan Commission** (February 1983) found Sharon indirectly responsible, and he left the defense ministry. Begin resigned (1983). The war led to the security zone in southern Lebanon (1985–2000) and the rise of Hezbollah. — [Wikipedia: 1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War); [Wikipedia: Sabra and Shatila massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabra_and_Shatila_massacre); [Wikipedia: Kahan Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahan_Commission)
- **Economy — the bank shares crisis (October 1983):** Banks had propped up their own share prices. When the scheme collapsed, the government nationalized the major banks in effect. The Bejski Commission (1986) led to resignations. Inflation reached about 450% (1984). — [Wikipedia: 1983 Israel bank stock crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Israel_bank_stock_crisis)

#### ERA 12 — Unity governments, stabilization and the First Intifada (1984–1992)
- **GOV:** a rotation-based National Unity Government (Peres 1984–86, Shamir 1986–88), then Shamir governments. — [Wikipedia: Twenty-first government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-first_government_of_Israel)
- **DP — the 1985 Economic Stabilization Plan (July 1985):** Peres, Finance Minister Modai, and economists (Bruno, Fischer as US adviser). Measures: budget cuts, devaluation, a wage–price freeze agreed with the Histadrut, and the end of deficit monetization (the 1985 "no printing" law). Inflation fell from about 450% to about 20%. Alternatives at the time were dollarization (Aridor's 1983 proposal, which leaked and fed panic) and gradualism. — [Wikipedia: 1985 Israel Economic Stabilization Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Israel_Economic_Stabilization_Plan)
- **Scandals:** the Kav 300 (Bus 300) affair (1984; cover-up of Shin Bet killings of captured hijackers); the Jonathan Pollard spy case (1985); the cancellation of the Lavi fighter (1987). — [Wikipedia: Kav 300 affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kav_300_affair); [Wikipedia: Jonathan Pollard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Pollard)
- **DP — the London Agreement (April 1987):** Foreign Minister Peres and King Hussein agreed secretly on an international conference. PM Shamir vetoed it. **Branch:** the "Jordanian option" succeeds, and Palestinian representation goes through Jordan. — [Wikipedia: London Agreement (1987)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Agreement)
- **First Intifada (December 1987–1993):** a mass Palestinian uprising (strikes, boycotts, stone-throwing). Rabin's "break their bones" policy was reported [CONTESTED wording]. Hamas was founded (1987). Jordan disengaged from the West Bank (1988), and the PNC declared independence and implicitly accepted 242 (1988). About 1,100–1,400 Palestinians and about 160–180 Israelis were killed [B'Tselem-type figures vary]. — [Wikipedia: First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada)
- **Soviet aliyah:** about 1 million immigrants from 1989 to the early 2000s. It caused a housing crisis (caravans) and required US loan guarantees. The $10 billion guarantee dispute with Bush and Baker (1991–92) was tied to settlement freezes. — [Wikipedia: 1990s post-Soviet aliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_post-Soviet_aliyah)
- **DP — Gulf War (January–February 1991):** Iraq fired 39–40+ Scuds at Israel. Shamir chose not to retaliate, at US request, to keep the coalition with Arab states intact. Patriot batteries were deployed and gas masks distributed. Alternative: an IAF strike on western Iraq. — [Wikipedia: Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War)
- **Others:** Operation Solomon (May 1991, about 14,300 Ethiopian Jews in 36 hours); the **Madrid Conference** (October 1991); the "dirty trick" (1990), Peres's failed attempt to topple the Shamir government, which led to the direct-election reform of 1992. — [Wikipedia: Operation Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Solomon); [Wikipedia: Madrid Conference of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Conference_of_1991)

#### ERA 13 — Oslo, the Rabin assassination and the peace-process decade (1992–2000)
- **EVT:** the **Oslo Accords** (Declaration of Principles, 13 September 1993; Oslo II, 1995); the **Cave of the Patriarchs massacre** (February 1994, 29 Palestinians killed by Baruch Goldstein); Hamas suicide bombing waves (1994–96); the **Israel–Jordan peace treaty** (October 1994); the **assassination of Rabin** (4 November 1995) by Yigal Amir; Netanyahu narrowly elected in the first direct PM election (1996); the Hasmonean Tunnel riots (1996); the Hebron Protocol (1997); the Wye River Memorandum (1998); Barak elected (1999); the **Versailles wedding hall disaster** (24 May 1999, 23 killed in a floor collapse; the "Pal-Kal" construction method was banned); the unilateral **withdrawal from southern Lebanon** (May 2000); the **Camp David Summit** (July 2000) failed. — [Wikipedia: Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords); [Wikipedia: Assassination of Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Yitzhak_Rabin); [Wikipedia: Versailles wedding hall disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versailles_wedding_hall_disaster); [Wikipedia: 2000 Camp David Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
- **[CONTESTED — Camp David 2000]:** The Israeli and US narrative (Barak, Ross, Clinton) blames Arafat for rejecting a generous offer. Robert Malley and Hussein Agha, and Palestinian negotiators, argue the offer did not provide a viable, contiguous state or resolve Jerusalem and refugees, and that the process itself was flawed. — [Wikipedia: 2000 Camp David Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Camp_David_Summit)
- **Economy:** the 1990s tech boom (the Yozma program, 1993; Soviet engineers); privatizations; the 1990s influx of foreign workers after closures on Palestinian labor. — [Wikipedia: Yozma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yozma)

#### ERA 14 — Second Intifada, Disengagement and Lebanon 2006 (2000–2009)
- **Second (al-Aqsa) Intifada (September 2000–about 2005):** It began after Sharon's Temple Mount visit and the violent dispersal of protests. About 1,000+ Israelis and about 3,000+ Palestinians were killed. Major bombings: the Dolphinarium (June 2001, 21 killed), Sbarro (August 2001, 15), and the Park Hotel Passover massacre (March 2002, 30), which led to **Operation Defensive Shield** and the separation barrier (from 2002). — [Wikipedia: Second Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Intifada); [Wikipedia: Operation Defensive Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Defensive_Shield)
- **October 2000 events:** 13 Arab citizens (12 Israeli citizens plus 1 Gazan) were killed by police during protests. The **Or Commission** (2003) criticized the police and decades of state neglect. — [Wikipedia: October 2000 events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2000_events)
- **Diplomacy:** the Arab Peace Initiative (2002), the Roadmap (2003), the Geneva Initiative (2003, unofficial).
- **Economy:** the 2001–03 recession; Netanyahu's reforms as finance minister in 2003 (cuts to welfare, pension reform, Bachar reform of the capital markets).
- **DP — Disengagement (August 2005):** Sharon unilaterally evacuated 21 Gaza settlements and 4 in the northern West Bank, about 8,000–9,000 settlers. Likud members rejected the plan in a 2004 referendum, and Sharon proceeded anyway. He then split Likud and founded Kadima (November 2005). Alternatives: a negotiated withdrawal with Abbas, or staying. Contested outcome: Hamas won the PA election (January 2006) and seized Gaza (June 2007), followed by an Israeli and Egyptian blockade. — [Wikipedia: Israeli disengagement from Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_disengagement_from_Gaza)
- **Second Lebanon War (12 July–14 August 2006):** It began with a Hezbollah cross-border raid and abduction. 121 IDF soldiers and 44 Israeli civilians were killed, and about 1,100+ Lebanese (mostly civilians per Lebanese counts). UNSC Resolution 1701 ended it. The **Winograd Commission** criticized Olmert, Peretz and Halutz. Halutz resigned. — [Wikipedia: 2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War); [Wikipedia: Winograd Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winograd_Commission)
- **Others:** the capture of Gilad Shalit (June 2006); the Syrian reactor strike (September 2007; Operation Orchard); Annapolis (2007) and the Olmert offer to Abbas (2008), which got no response [CONTESTED]; Olmert resigned over corruption (2008) and was later imprisoned; President Katsav was convicted of rape (2010); **Cast Lead** (December 2008–January 2009; about 1,400 Palestinians and 13 Israelis killed; the Goldstone Report). — [Wikipedia: Gaza War (2008–2009)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_(2008%E2%80%932009)); [Wikipedia: Operation Orchard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Outside_the_Box)

#### ERA 15 — Netanyahu's long premiership, Gaza rounds, Abraham Accords (2009–2021)
- **EVT:** the Gaza flotilla raid (May 2010, 9–10 Turkish activists killed; crisis with Turkey); the **Carmel forest fire** (December 2010, 44 killed, mostly prison service cadets; international firefighting aid; Comptroller criticism of the fire service); the **2011 social justice protests** (housing and cost of living; the Trajtenberg Committee); the **Shalit deal** (October 2011, 1,027 prisoners released, including Yahya Sinwar); Pillar of Defense (2012); **Protective Edge** (2014; 73 Israelis and about 2,250 Palestinians killed); JCPOA (2015) and Netanyahu's 2015 speech to Congress against it; the US embassy moved to Jerusalem (May 2018); the **Nation-State Law** (July 2018); five elections (April 2019–November 2022); the indictment of Netanyahu (2019; Cases 1000, 2000 and 4000; trial from 2020). — [Wikipedia: Gaza flotilla raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_flotilla_raid); [Wikipedia: Mount Carmel forest fire (2010)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel_forest_fire_(2010)); [Wikipedia: 2011 Israeli social justice protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Israeli_social_justice_protests); [Wikipedia: Gilad Shalit prisoner exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilad_Shalit_prisoner_exchange); [Wikipedia: 2014 Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Gaza_War); [Wikipedia: Basic Law: Israel as the Nation-State of the Jewish People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law:_Israel_as_the_Nation-State_of_the_Jewish_People); [Wikipedia: 2019–2022 Israeli political crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932022_Israeli_political_crisis)
- **COVID-19:** the first lockdown (March 2020), three lockdowns in total; the world-leading Pfizer vaccine rollout (from December 2020), in which Israel traded rapid supply for data sharing. — [Wikipedia: COVID-19 pandemic in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Israel)
- **Abraham Accords (September 2020):** normalization with the UAE and Bahrain, then Morocco and Sudan. Netanyahu's West Bank annexation plan was suspended in exchange. — [Wikipedia: Abraham Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Accords)
- **2021:** the **Meron crowd crush** (30 April 2021, 45 killed at Lag BaOmer; a state commission blamed Netanyahu, Ohana and others for "personal responsibility"); **May 2021** (Guardian of the Walls in Gaza plus Jewish–Arab riots in mixed cities such as Lod, Acre and Ramle); the **Bennett–Lapid government** (June 2021–2022), the first with an Arab party (Ra'am) as a coalition member. — [Wikipedia: 2021 Meron crowd crush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Meron_crowd_crush); [Wikipedia: 2021 Israel–Palestine crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_crisis); [Wikipedia: Thirty-sixth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_government_of_Israel)

#### ERA 16 — Judicial overhaul crisis (December 2022–6 October 2023)
- **GOV:** Netanyahu's 37th government: Likud, Shas, UTJ, Religious Zionism, Otzma Yehudit, Noam.
- **EVT:** Justice Minister Levin announced the overhaul (January 2023): changing the judicial selection committee, a Knesset override clause, and abolishing the reasonableness test. Mass weekly protests followed, along with reservist refusal threats. Netanyahu fired Defense Minister Gallant (26 March 2023), which triggered a spontaneous general strike; the firing was reversed and the legislation paused. The **reasonableness amendment** passed (24 July 2023). The Supreme Court struck it down (January 2024; see Key Question 6). There were credit warnings and a slowdown in tech investment. — [Wikipedia: 2023 Israeli judicial reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform); [IDI tag on the reasonableness ruling](https://idi.org.il/tags/1463)
- **DP:** Compromise (the Herzog framework of March 2023, which the coalition rejected) versus pushing ahead. This is a model case of a domestic-legitimacy dilemma with security spillover, because the IDF and intelligence warned about readiness.

#### ERA 17 — October 7 and the multi-front war (7 October 2023–October 2026)
See Key Question 6.

### Inferences
- **Pre-state play (Eras 1–5)** should use a *legitimacy and influence* model. The player persuades the empire, the Diaspora donors and the rival factions (Revisionists, dissident militias, religious groups). It should not use a command model. Resources are certificates, donor money, land dunams and Haganah strength.
- **Statehood (Era 6+)** shifts to a *coalition-arithmetic plus institutions* model. From 1977 the left–right balance is competitive. From the mid-1990s judicial review (the Mizrahi Bank ruling, 1995) adds a court actor. After 2019, election instability is a core mechanic.
- Natural "chapter" groupings for a campaign: (a) 1882–1948 *Building*; (b) 1948–1967 *Founding*; (c) 1967–1985 *Holding*; (d) 1985–2000 *Opening*; (e) 2000–2022 *Managing*; (f) 2023–2026 *Reckoning*.

### Gaps
- Exact figures for immigration waves and casualties differ between sources. Pre-2023 figures here were drawn from standard reference entries and not re-verified against Shapira, Morris or Gilbert in this session.
- I did not retrieve Israel State Archives documents directly. Cabinet protocols (for example the May 1967 and October 1973 cabinet discussions) are published there and would strengthen the decision-point accuracy.

## Key Question 3: Cold War and great-power alignment shifts

### Takeaway
Israel's patrons changed in stages:
- **USSR and Czechoslovakia** (arms, 1948);
- non-alignment attempts (1948–50);
- tilt to the West after Korea (1950);
- **France** (the main arms supplier, 1954–67; Dimona);
- the **US**, gradually after 1962 (Hawk missiles) and decisively after 1967–73;
- with **Africa outreach** (1958–73), which collapsed after 1973;
- the **Soviet Jewry** campaign (1960s–89) as a soft-power issue;
- the post-Cold War restoration of ties with the USSR/Russia, China and India (1991–92).

### Cited Findings
- The USSR voted for partition (Gromyko speech, May 1947). Czechoslovak arms (Operation Balak, 1948) were decisive. The Soviet Union recognized Israel de jure on 17 May 1948. — [Wikipedia: Israel–Russia relations / Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Soviet_Union_relations); [Wikipedia: Czechoslovak arms to Israel 1947–1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_arms_to_Israel_1947%E2%80%931949)
- The Soviet tilt to the Arabs followed the Doctors' Plot and the bombing of the Soviet legation (1953, relations briefly severed) and the Czech–Egyptian arms deal (1955). The USSR broke relations in June 1967 and restored them in 1991. — [Wikipedia: Israel–Soviet Union relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Soviet_Union_relations)
- The French alliance gave Mystère and Mirage jets, the Sèvres collusion (1956) and the Dimona reactor. De Gaulle imposed an arms embargo in 1967–69 (including the Cherbourg boats affair, 1969). — [Wikipedia: France–Israel relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France%E2%80%93Israel_relations); [Wikipedia: Cherbourg Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherbourg_Project)
- The US moved from an arms embargo (1948) to selling Hawk missiles (1962, Kennedy), then Skyhawks and Phantoms (1968). Turning points were the airlift of 1973, the Memorandum of Understanding on military aid (from 1999; the 2016 MOU is worth $38 billion for 2019–28), and the strategic cooperation agreement (1981). — [Wikipedia: Israel–United States relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_relations)
- Africa: MASHAV (1958) development aid to newly independent states. Most African states broke relations after 1973, and most restored them after 1990. — [Wikipedia: Israel–Africa relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa%E2%80%93Israel_relations); [Wikipedia: MASHAV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MASHAV)
- Soviet Jewry: refuseniks (Sharansky); the Jackson–Vanik Amendment (1974); the Freedom Sunday rally (1987); the mass exodus from 1989. Israel lobbied the US to restrict refugee entry so that émigrés would come to Israel [CONTESTED policy]. — [Wikipedia: Refusenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refusenik); [Wikipedia: Jackson–Vanik amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson%E2%80%93Vanik_amendment)
- Other alignments: the periphery doctrine (Iran under the Shah, Turkey, Ethiopia; 1950s–79); ties with apartheid South Africa (1970s–80s) [CONTESTED in scope]; China and India relations established in 1992. — [Wikipedia: Periphery doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periphery_doctrine)

### Inferences
- A game "patron meter" could track *arms supply reliability* and *diplomatic cover (UN vetoes)* per great power. Key branching points: 1950 (Korea vote), 1956 (collusion with declining empires), 1962/68 (US arms), 1967 (French embargo), 1991 (end of the Cold War).

### Gaps
- I did not obtain detailed archival sources for Israeli cabinet debates on the 1950 Korea alignment in this session.

## Key Question 6: 2023–October 2026 — October 7, the multi-front war, and the 2026 election

### Takeaway
This is the most volatile era. Its phases:
- the October 7 massacre and hostage-taking;
- a Gaza war of about two years, ending in a **Trump-brokered ceasefire on 10 October 2025** and the release of all living hostages (13 October 2025);
- the multi-front campaign against Hezbollah, Syria, the Houthis and Iran;
- the **Twelve-Day War with Iran** (June 2025);
- a much larger **US–Israel war with Iran from 28 February 2026**, which killed Khamenei and is listed as ongoing or unresolved;
- Lebanon fighting again and direct Israel–Lebanon talks (2026);
- Gaza "Phase Two" governance stalled over Hamas disarmament;
- domestically, the Netanyahu pardon request, the dispute over the October 7 inquiry, the Haredi draft crisis, and an on-schedule **election on 27 October 2026**.
